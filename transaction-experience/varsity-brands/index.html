Varsity Spirit Corporation — founding, IPO, and the 1997 Riddell Sports acquisition

Two-step public tender offer + back-end merger (cash), financed with high-yield notes

Announced / closed

Merger agreement May 5, 1997; tender offer completed June 19, 1997; merger consummated July 25, 1997

Headline value

$18.90 per share cash; ~$85.6m for 4,511,415 shares (98.5% of Varsity Spirit common); ~$91m all-in including options/expenses

Parties

Riddell Sports Inc. (acquirer) / Varsity Spirit Corp. (NASDAQ)

Structure

  • Two-step: cash tender offer at $18.90/share closed June 19, 1997 taking 98.5%; back-end merger July 25, 1997 cashed out the remainder at the same price.
  • Riddell Sports renamed itself Varsity Brands, Inc. effective August 24, 2001 (SEC former-name record, CIK 874786).
  • Pre-history: Jeff Webb founded Universal Cheerleaders Association in 1974 at age 23 with $80,000 raised via $5,000 limited-partnership units plus $5,000 of his own money. Revenue: $2m (1979) → ~$10m (late 1980s) → $20m+ (1989) → $28.1m (1991) → $33.8m (1992) → $41.6m (1993). A late-1989 LBO by the original limited partners sold two-thirds to investors; the investor group took the company public in 1992 as Varsity Spirit Corp. Webb received ~$7.7m and kept ~13%; IPO proceeds retired all debt.
  • Varsity Spirit at acquisition: 1996 revenue $88.4m, net income $5.2m.

Financing

  • $115,000,000 aggregate principal amount of 10½% Senior Notes, issued June 19, 1997 (registered via Form S-4 exchange offer, S-4/A filed Aug 7, 1997).
  • New secured credit facility.
  • Proceeds funded the acquisition, refinanced the prior credit agreement, and paid transaction costs.
  • Initial purchasers: NationsBanc Capital Markets and First Chicago Capital Markets.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

Not broken out in the S-4/A summary reviewed unverified.

Advisers

Legal — Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom LLP.

Key people

  • Jeffrey G. Webb — founder, Varsity Spirit (1974); continued as the driving executive across four decades of ownership changes
  • Robert M. Nederlander — CEO, Riddell Sports; held ~44% personally plus ~10% via M.L.C. Partners L.P. as of May 1997
  • John M. Nichols — Varsity Brands executive; rollover participant in the 2003 take-private

Regulatory and litigation

Customary HSR; no challenge recorded.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Created a combined football-equipment + cheerleading company. Riddell Sports → Varsity Brands, Inc. (2001). The two halves were separated in 2003.

  • John M. Nichols — executive, rollover buyer, 13E-3 filing person
  • Leonard Green & Partners (Green Equity Investors IV) — control sponsor

SEC Rule 13e-3 disclosure regime; seven Schedule 13E-3 amendments. No litigation recorded in the filings reviewed.

Collegiate Pacific / Sport Supply Group → ONCAP take-private (2010)

Take-private LBO with management and existing-shareholder rollover; go-shop

Announced / closed

Merger agreement announced March 15–16, 2010; closed August 2010

Headline value

$13.55 per share cash; ONCAP entry EV reported at ~7.2x EBITDA; equity value ~$147–184m depending on the measure [unverified — total not disclosed in the merger press release]

Parties

ONCAP Management Partners, L.P. (mid-market arm of Onex Corporation) / Sport Supply Group, Inc. (NASDAQ: RBI) / CBT Holdings (Andell Holdings affiliate, ~16% stake) and management (rollover)

Structure

  • Pre-history: Collegiate Pacific (Adam Blumenfeld / Mike Blumenfeld) acquired 53.2% of Sport Supply Group in July 2005, later going to full ownership, then renamed itself Sport Supply Group, Inc. BSN Sports was originally a ~$40m division inside Sport Supply Group (1996), reaching ~$250m combined by 2006.
  • 2010 merger: one-step merger; CBT Holdings (Andell) and management exchanged shares for equity in the purchaser entity rather than cash — a rollover, not a cash-out.
  • 30-day go-shop (extendable by 15 days), with a $3–6m termination fee for a superior proposal accepted during go-shop and $6–10m reverse fee if ONCAP’s financing failed.
  • Not subject to a financing condition, but ONCAP retained unilateral termination rights tied to financing availability.
  • Under ONCAP (Aug 2010 – June 2013) the company completed nine acquisitions and consolidated its go-to-market under the BSN SPORTS brand. At entry it served 110,000+ institutional customers via catalogs, websites and direct sales.

Financing

Not disclosed in the merger press release; a committed-financing structure with a reverse fee backstop [tranches unverified].

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

Private post-closing; no public financials unverified.

Advisers

  • Sport Supply Group — Houlihan Lokey Howard & Zukin Capital, Inc. (financial); Vinson & Elkins LLP (legal)
  • ONCAP — O’Melveny & Myers LLP (legal)

Key people

  • Adam Blumenfeld — CEO, Collegiate Pacific → Sport Supply Group → BSN SPORTS (from 2007) → CEO, Varsity Brands (from 2017)
  • Mike Blumenfeld — founder of Collegiate Pacific; former CEO; Chairman
  • Andell Holdings / CBT Holdings — rollover shareholder

Regulatory and litigation

Customary HSR; go-shop produced no superior proposal.

Outcome and post-deal performance

ONCAP exited to Herff Jones in June 2013 after ~3 years. Later reporting characterizes the exit as roughly a 7.3x multiple of invested capital on the platform value trajectory ($184m entry → $1.5bn 2014 Charlesbank valuation of the combined group) — this is a comparison across different asset perimeters and should not be read as ONCAP’s MOIC unverified.

Herff Jones, Inc. — acquisition of Varsity Brands (2011)

ESOP-owned strategic acquires a sponsor-owned platform

Announced / closed

Announced June 15, 2011 / Closed August 2011

Headline value

Not disclosed

Parties

Herff Jones, Inc. (100% employee-owned via ESOP since 1995) / VBR Holding Corporation (parent of Varsity Brands), majority-held by an affiliate of Leonard Green & Partners, L.P.

Structure

  • VBR Holding Corporation merged with a Herff Jones subsidiary; Varsity Brands continued to operate independently under its own Board within the Herff Jones umbrella.
  • Jeff Webb remained Chairman & CEO of Varsity Brands; Joe Slaughter remained Chairman & CEO of Herff Jones.
  • Varsity at the time: 900 full-time employees plus ~4,000 seasonal summer camp staff; offices in Memphis, Dallas and Los Angeles.
  • Herff Jones history: founded Jan 6, 1920 in Indianapolis by Harry J. Herff and Randall H. Jones; acquired by Carnation (Nestlé) 1974; management buyout led by A.J. Hackl 1985; ESOP established 1989, 100% employee-owned by 1995. Prior acquisitions: Collegiate Cap & Gown (1979), Dieges & Clust (1980, maker of the Heisman Trophy).

Financing

Not disclosed unverified.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

Private; not disclosed unverified.

Advisers

  • Herff Jones — Blackstone Advisory Partners L.P. (financial); Barnes & Thornburg (legal)
  • Leonard Green & Partners — Rothschild and Jefferies & Company, Inc. (financial); Latham & Watkins LLP (legal)

Key people

  • Joe Slaughter — Chairman & CEO, Herff Jones
  • Jeff Webb — founder, Chairman & CEO, Varsity Brands (retained)
  • Leonard Green & Partners — exiting sponsor

Regulatory and litigation

Herff Jones’ prior attempt to buy American Achievement Group Holding Corp. had been abandoned December 5, 2008 after an FTC Bureau of Competition investigation concluded the deal was anticompetitive in commemorative products, specifically class rings — the FTC issued a public statement December 8, 2008. That failed deal (reported at up to ~$700m) is directly relevant: it explains why Herff Jones pivoted to adjacent categories (cheer in 2011, team sports in 2013) rather than consolidating its direct rival. American Achievement was ultimately sold to Jostens / Visant under a stock purchase agreement announced November 19, 2013.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Created the school-spirit + achievement platform later rebranded Varsity Brands.

Varsity Brands — 2018 acquisition by Bain Capital Private Equity

Sponsor-to-sponsor secondary buyout

Announced / closed

Announced June 19, 2018 / Closed Q3 2018

Headline value

~$2.5bn (reported; not disclosed by the parties)

Parties

Bain Capital Private Equity / Charlesbank Capital Partners and funds managed and advised by Partners Group (sellers) / Varsity Brands

Structure

  • Purchase of the three-brand platform: BSN Sports, Varsity Spirit, Herff Jones.
  • Varsity Brands at the time: founded 1974; 9,000 employees and independent representatives; serving schools, universities and professional/collegiate teams.
  • Charlesbank retained a minority stake — evidenced by its co-appearance as a seller alongside Bain in the 2024 KKR exit.

Financing

  • Original 2018 LBO tranches not disclosed by the parties unverified.
  • The capital structure as it stood immediately before the 2024 KKR takeout (after a Nov 2023 refinancing) was: $1.132bn first-lien term loan due Dec 2026; $450m senior secured first-lien notes due Dec 2026; $620m second-lien term loan due April 2027; $350m ABL revolver due June 2026.
  • Moody’s, Nov 7, 2023: assigned B2 to the new $450m first-lien notes due Dec 2026 at Varsity Brands Holding Co., Inc.; affirmed B3 CFR, B3 PDR, B2 on the existing first-lien term loan due 2026; withdrew B2 ratings on the repaid 2024 term loan (~$305m) and 2024 notes ($44m); outlook stable. Proceeds also paid down ~$95m of the revolver (which had $245m drawn on a $350m facility as of July 1, 2023). Leverage: 6.9x debt/EBITDA for the twelve months ended July 1, 2023, projected to 6.7x by year-end 2024.
  • S&P had rated the credit B+ roughly nine years earlier, migrating to B- by 2024 — a full three-notch deterioration across the Charlesbank → Bain ownership arc.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

Private; not disclosed unverified.

Advisers

  • Sellers (Charlesbank / Partners Group) — Jefferies LLC (lead financial advisor); Goldman Sachs and Peter J. Solomon (co-advisors); Goodwin Procter (legal); PwC (accounting)

Varsity Brands — antitrust and abuse litigation (2020–2026)

N/A — litigation overlay material to valuation across the Bain and KKR transactions

Announced / closed

Complaints filed 2020; settlements October 2023 and May 2024; abuse litigation ongoing into 2025–2026

Headline value

$43.5m (gyms/spectators class) + $82.5m (athletes/parents class) = $126.0m in aggregate antitrust settlements

Parties

Varsity Brands, LLC and subsidiaries; Bain Capital Private Equity; Charlesbank Capital Partners; Jeff Webb (individually); U.S. All Star Federation, Inc. (USASF); classes of all-star cheer gyms, spectators, and athletes/parents

Structure of the claims

  • Court: U.S. District Court for the Western District of Tennessee.
  • Fusion Elite All Stars, et al. v. Varsity Brands, LLC, et al., No. 2:20-cv-02600-SHL-cgc — Sherman Act monopolization of the All-Star Cheer Events market via three alleged mechanisms: (i) serial acquisition of rival event producers; (ii) exclusive patronage/rebate agreements binding gyms; (iii) capture of the governing body (USASF) to exclude competitors. Two certified classes: All-Star Cheer Gyms and Spectators.
  • Jones, et al. v. Bain Capital Private Equity, et al. (consolidated May 2020) — parallel claims on behalf of athletes/parents who paid for camps, competitions and apparel; named the private equity owners directly.
  • The “Stay to Play”/“Stay Smart” hotel-booking policy — under which attendance at Varsity events required booking through Varsity-designated hotels, with Varsity receiving rebates — was a central alleged overcharge mechanism.

Settlements

  • Fusion Elite — $43.5m cash, plus prospective injunctive relief unwinding key exclusionary conduct. Preliminary approval late 2022; final approval October 2023.
  • Jones — $82.5m, presented to the court in May 2024 (i.e., during KKR’s diligence and immediately before signing). Allocation: $27.5m plaintiffs’ attorneys’ fees + $9.2m litigation costs; class representative service awards of $25,000–$50,000 each; remainder to class members.
  • Injunctive relief in Jones: (i) elimination of the requirement that cheerleaders attend Varsity camps to qualify for national championships; (ii) at least 35% of Varsity competitions must not require Varsity-approved hotel stays; (iii) USASF prohibited from sharing confidential competition data with Varsity.

Abuse litigation

  • September/October 2022: multiple federal suits filed alleging systemic sexual abuse of minors in all-star cheerleading, naming Varsity Brands, Varsity Spirit, USASF, USA Cheer, and Varsity’s private equity owners. Reported claims of up to $200m against Varsity and its PE owner.
  • The scandal spread across at least five states by late 2022.
  • Litigation continued into 2025, with new cheerleader sex-abuse suits filed (including in Georgia) after a period of relative quiet.
  • No global settlement of the abuse claims has been reported [status as of the sources reviewed].

Advisors / counsel

  • Class counsel in Fusion Elite: Berger Montague (Co-Lead Class Counsel, Eric L. Cramer); DiCello Levitt; Justice Catalyst Law
  • Settlement administrator: Angeion Group

Key people

  • Jeff Webb — named individually as a defendant in Jones
  • Bain Capital and Charlesbank — named as defendants, an unusually direct extension of antitrust liability to financial sponsors

Outcome

The antitrust exposure was substantially resolved before KKR’s August 2024 closing — the $82.5m Jones settlement was before the court in May 2024, contemporaneous with the sale process. Abuse claims remain a live contingent liability under KKR ownership.
