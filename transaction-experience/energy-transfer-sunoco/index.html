Energy Transfer Complex — Structure and Precedent Transactions (context)

MLP sponsor structure; serial midstream roll-up; busted-merger litigation

Announced / closed

Various, 2016–2025

Headline value

~$24.9bn of announced acquisition value across the five transactions below, plus a $601.5m adverse judgment

Parties

Energy Transfer LP (NYSE: ET) and affiliates; Sunoco LP (NYSE: SUN); USA Compression Partners (NYSE: USAC); NuStar, WTG, Crestwood, Enable, Williams

Structure

Sponsor architecture (as of May 2025)

  • Energy Transfer LP holds the general partner interest, the incentive distribution rights (IDRs), and approximately 21% of the outstanding common units of Sunoco LP — so ET captures both GP economics and the IDR waterfall from every Sunoco acquisition, including Parkland.
  • ET also holds the GP interests and ~39% of the common units of USA Compression Partners (USAC).
  • ET operates 135,000+ miles of pipeline across 44 states and moves approximately 30% of total US natural gas transportation. 2025E Adjusted EBITDA mix: Midstream 27%, Intrastate & Interstate 19%, NGL & Refined Products 21%, Crude Oil 13%, SUN/USAC/Other 20%. Annualized distribution $1.31/unit as of May 2025.

Sunoco LP — NuStar Energy L.P. (2024)

  • Announced 22 January 2024, closed 3 May 2024. ~$7.3bn including assumed debt, all-equity at 0.400 Sunoco common units per NuStar common unit. 24% premium to 30-day VWAPs as of 19 January 2024. Unanimous board approval both sides; NuStar unitholder approval required.
  • Synergies at least $150m run-rate by year 3; 10%+ DCF/unit accretion by year 3. Sunoco announced a 4% quarterly distribution increase on closing.
  • Advisors — Sunoco: Truist Securities (exclusive financial advisor); Truist and Bank of America (committed financing); Weil, Gotshal & Manges and Vinson & Elkins (legal). NuStar: Barclays (exclusive financial advisor); Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz and Sidley Austin (legal).

Energy Transfer — WTG Midstream (2024)

  • Announced 28 May 2024, closed Q3 2024. $3.25bn: $2.45bn cash + ~50.8 million newly issued ET common units.
  • Assets: 6,000+ miles of gas gathering pipelines; eight operating processing plants (~1.3 Bcf/d); two under construction (~0.4 Bcf/d); 20% interest in the BANGL NGL Pipeline (425 miles, 125,000 Bbls/d).
  • Accretion: ~$0.04/unit DCF in 2025, ~$0.07/unit by 2027.
  • Advisors — ET: RBC Capital Markets (financial), Vinson & Elkins (legal). WTG: Jefferies LLC (financial), Sidley Austin (legal).

Energy Transfer — Crestwood Equity Partners (2023)

  • Announced 16 August 2023, closed 3 November 2023. ~$7.1bn including $3.3bn of assumed debt, all-equity at 2.07 ET common units per Crestwood common unit. Crestwood holders received ~6.5% of ET’s outstanding common units.
  • Synergies: at least $40m annual run-rate cost synergies.
  • Advisors — ET: BofA Securities (sole financial advisor), Kirkland & Ellis (legal). Crestwood: Intrepid Partners and Evercore (financial), Vinson & Elkins (legal).

Energy Transfer — Enable Midstream Partners (2021)

  • Announced 17 February 2021, closed December 2021. ~$7.2bn all-equity at 0.8595 ET common units per Enable common unit; each Enable Series A preferred unit exchanged for 0.0265 ET Series G preferred units; $10m cash for Enable’s general partner.
  • Sellers: CenterPoint Energy and OGE Energy, together ~79.2% of Enable’s common units, both bound by support agreements. The transaction began CenterPoint’s exit from midstream.
  • Synergies: more than $100m annual run-rate cost and efficiency synergies, plus commercial synergies from integrating Anadarko G&P with Gulf Coast fractionation.
  • Advisors — ET: Citi and RBC Capital Markets (financial), Latham & Watkins (legal). Enable: Goldman Sachs (financial), Vinson & Elkins (legal). Enable Conflicts Committee: Intrepid Partners (financial), Richards, Layton & Finger (legal).

Energy Transfer / Williams Companies — busted merger and litigation (2015–2023)

  • The ~$33bn 2015–16 “Up-C” merger of Energy Transfer Equity and Williams contemplated ~$6.05bn cash plus stock in a new entity, Energy Transfer Corp LP, with former Williams shareholders holding ~81%.
  • ETE terminated after tax counsel Latham & Watkins stated it could not deliver the required Section 721(a) tax opinion. The Delaware courts held the failure of that condition precedent excused ETE’s obligation to close — but separately found ETE materially breached its covenants through a Preferred Offering that preferenced ETE insiders.
  • Court of Chancery, December 2021: awarded Williams the $410m WPZ Termination Fee Reimbursement, plus attorneys’ fees under a 15% contingency arrangement and prejudgment interest compounded quarterly.
  • Total judgment $601.5m as of August 2022 (break fee plus more than $190m of interest and fees).
  • Delaware Supreme Court affirmed 10 October 2023 (Energy Transfer, LP v. The Williams Companies, Inc., No. 391, 2022), leaving a judgment of over $600m against Energy Transfer.

Financing

Enable and Crestwood were all-equity (no financing). WTG was $2.45bn cash plus units, funded from ET liquidity. NuStar was all-equity with Truist/BofA committed financing available. Parkland financing is set out in the section above.

Recorded in ET’s FY2021 (Enable), FY2023 (Crestwood), FY2024 (WTG) and Sunoco LP’s FY2024 (NuStar) Forms 10-K. [Individual goodwill figures not verified for this document — see EDGAR filing index links below.]

Regulatory and litigation

HSR clearance on each acquisition; FERC where interstate assets transferred; unitholder votes at NuStar, Crestwood and Enable. The Williams matter is the significant litigation exposure — a $601.5m adverse judgment affirmed on appeal.

Outcome and post-deal performance

The complex executed roughly $24.9bn of announced acquisitions from 2021 to 2025 (Enable $7.2bn, Crestwood $7.1bn, NuStar $7.3bn, WTG $3.25bn) plus Parkland’s $9.1bn at Sunoco, building one of the largest integrated midstream and fuel distribution platforms in North America. Energy Transfer captures value from each Sunoco deal through its GP interest, IDRs and ~21% LP stake. The Williams judgment stands as the principal offsetting cost of the era’s dealmaking.

Sunoco LP — Acquisition of Parkland Corporation

Cross-border MLP roll-up via Canadian plan of arrangement; part-cash / part-equity with a novel Up-C-style corporate vehicle; shareholder-activism-driven sale process

Announced / closed

Announced 5 May 2025; Parkland shareholder approval 24 June 2025; closed 31 October 2025

Headline value

~US$9.1bn including assumed debt; C$44.00 per Parkland share headline cash election (≈C$43.33 blended value at announcement), a 25% premium

Parties

Sunoco LP (NYSE: SUN) — buyer, sponsored by Energy Transfer LP (NYSE: ET) / Parkland Corporation (TSX: PKI) — target / Simpson Oil Limited — Parkland’s 19.8% shareholder and catalyst

Structure

  • Canadian plan of arrangement under Alberta law, requiring a special resolution and final approval of the Court of King’s Bench of Alberta.
  • Three-way election per Parkland share, subject to proration against aggregate caps of C$19.80 cash and 0.295 units per share:
    • Default/mixed: 0.295 SunocoCorp units + C$19.80 cash
    • All cash: C$44.00
    • All equity: ~0.536 SunocoCorp units
  • SunocoCorp, LLC — a newly formed Delaware limited liability company holding Sunoco LP units and treated as a corporation for US tax purposes. This solves the classic problem of paying MLP units to a broad Canadian retail shareholder base (K-1s, UBTI, foreign-holder complications) by handing them a corporate-taxed, 1099-style listed security instead. Listed on NYSE under “SUNC” from 6 November 2025.
  • Dividend equivalence: for two years post-close, SunocoCorp unitholders receive the same distribution equivalent as Sunoco LP common unitholders.
  • Final consideration paid: C$3,457,770,643.42 in cash plus 51,517,198 SunocoCorp units.
  • Parkland delisted from the TSX effective close of business 4 November 2025.
  • Canadian commitments (politically necessary for Investment Canada Act clearance): maintain Canadian headquarters in Calgary with significant employment; continue investment in the Burnaby Refinery for low-carbon fuel production; support infrastructure expansion.
  • Backstory — the Simpson Oil overhang: Simpson Oil Limited (Cayman Islands), Parkland’s largest shareholder at 19.8%, was bound by a Governance Agreement containing voting restrictions and standstill provisions. Simpson contended these had expired; Parkland’s board disputed this and threatened enforcement. Simpson litigated and won a court-adjudicated affirmation of its shareholder rights; Parkland stock rose 17% over two trading days on the ruling. In April 2024 Simpson publicly demanded a strategic review including a potential sale, citing –3.1% annualized 5-year TSR and 3.8% ROIC (below cost of capital) against peers averaging 23.2% annualized returns. Simpson also alleged the board had prematurely rejected unsolicited acquisition interest at a material premium in summer 2023 — reportedly from Sunoco itself. The board initially rejected the review, then relented; the process culminated in the 2025 sale. Simpson publicly attacked a subsequent AGM delay as a board tactic to retain control.
  • Parkland ran consent solicitations on its outstanding senior notes in connection with the acquisition.

Financing

  • US$2.65bn 364-day bridge term loan committed at announcement by Barclays and RBC Capital Markets, to fund the cash consideration.
  • Take-out financing priced 4 September 2025, settling 18 September 2025:
    • Senior notes — US$1.9bn upsized: $1.0bn at 5.625% due 2031 and $900m at 5.875% due 2034, both at par. Carried a special mandatory redemption if the acquisition did not close by 5 May 2026.
    • Preferred equity — US$1.5bn upsized (from 1.0m units): 1.5 million 7.875% Series A Fixed-Rate Reset Cumulative Redeemable Perpetual Preferred Units at $1,000 per unit. Fixed at 7.875% to 18 September 2030, then resetting to the five-year US Treasury rate + 4.230%. Semi-annual distributions from 18 March 2026; callable at $1,000 plus accrued after the first reset date.
    • Combined US$3.4bn of upsized notes and preferred equity.
  • Pre-closing, proceeds temporarily paid down revolver borrowings.

Sunoco LP’s FY2025 10-K carries the Parkland purchase price allocation; the transaction closed 31 October 2025, so allocation was preliminary at FY2025 year-end. [Specific goodwill and intangible figures not verified — see Sunoco LP EDGAR filing index in sources.]

Regulatory and litigation

  • Parkland shareholder approval: 24 June 2025 — 93.46% in favour (127,089,612 for; 8,890,026 against), passed as a special resolution.
  • Court of King’s Bench of Alberta final order approving the arrangement.
  • Investment Canada Act net-benefit approval — a significant hurdle given the Burnaby Refinery and Canadian fuel-security sensitivities; obtained ahead of closing.
  • Competition Act (Canada) clearance; HSR clearance in the US.
  • NYSE listing approval for SunocoCorp units.
  • Antecedent litigation: Simpson Oil v. Parkland over the Governance Agreement, resolved in Simpson Oil’s favour, which unlocked the sale process.

Outcome and post-deal performance

  • Closed 31 October 2025, creating the largest independent fuel distributor in the Americas, with combined enterprise value of approximately US$24.5bn.
  • Synergy target US$250m run-rate by year 3; management characterized it as “very doable” at announcement.
  • 10%+ accretion to distributable cash flow per common unit by year 3, immediately accretive.
  • Leverage: return to the 4x long-term target within 12–18 months post-close.
  • Parkland’s corporate web presence now redirects to Sunoco LP’s investor site, reflecting full integration.
  • Simpson Oil’s activist campaign — from the 2024 governance litigation through the strategic review to the 2025 sale — is the direct causal chain producing an exit at a 25% premium after five years of negative shareholder returns.

Sunoco LP — Acquisition of Offen Petroleum

Sponsor exit / bolt-on fuel distribution acquisition

Announced / closed

Announced 6 August 2026; expected close Q4 2026 (open as of September 2026)

Headline value

~US$600m, all cash

Parties

Sunoco LP (NYSE: SUN) — buyer / Offen Petroleum, a Denver-based fuel distributor and portfolio company of Court Square Capital Partners — target

Structure

  • All-cash acquisition, approximately $600 million.
  • Correction to the commonly cited account: this is a ~$600m deal announced August 2026, not a ~$1.0bn deal announced/closed in 2025.
  • Offen distributes approximately 2.5 billion gallons annually, serving approximately 7,000 customers and over 800 retail stations across the Midwest, Mountain West and Southwest United States.
  • Extends Sunoco’s fuel distribution footprint into Mountain West geographies complementary to the Parkland and NuStar assets; management framed it as creating “additional opportunities for organic growth and bolt-on acquisitions.”
  • Classic private-equity exit: Court Square Capital Partners realizing a platform investment into a strategic buyer that is itself consolidating the sector.

Financing

All-cash; specific financing sources not detailed in the announcement. Sunoco retains revolver capacity and post-Parkland balance-sheet flexibility.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

Not yet applicable — transaction had not closed as of September 2026.

Advisers

Not disclosed in the announcement press release.

  • Chris Cho — Director, Communications, Sunoco LP

Regulatory and litigation

Subject to regulatory approval (HSR clearance). No litigation disclosed.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Described as immediately accretive, increasing cash flow available for distribution growth and reinvestment. EBITDA multiple not disclosed; at ~$600m against 2.5bn gallons the implied price per gallon is roughly $0.24, in line with recent fuel-distribution comparables. Outcome pending close.
