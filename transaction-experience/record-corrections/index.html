Record corrections

  1. Cerberus/Dorel was terminated February 15, 2021, not rejected by shareholders in June 2021. The vote was cancelled before it happened; price had been raised to C$16.00 on February 1, 2021; aggregate value ~US$1.012 billion including debt and fees.
  2. Mosaic’s Cargill share issuance was 250,582,322 common + 5,458,955 Class B = 256,041,277 shares, per the closing 8-K — not ~175.3 million.
  3. Cove launched in 2018, not 2013. The 2013 event was Sub-Zero/Wolf’s largest product rollout.
  4. Wolf Range was founded in Los Angeles (1934, Al and Hyman Wolf), not Kansas City, and Sub-Zero bought only the residential (“Wolf Gourmet”) business; the commercial range business remained separate (Vulcan / ITW FEG).
  5. No Sub-Zero v. Whirlpool trade dress case was located. The genuine, well-documented trade dress litigation is Wolf Appliance, Inc. v. Viking Range Corp., W.D. Wis. 2010 (red knobs).
  6. Cannondale was US$190–200 million, earnings-contingent through June 30, 2008, not a flat US$200 million.
  7. Brunswick’s bicycle sale to Pacific Cycle was US$60 million; Brunswick’s FY2000 10-K says the sale completed during 2000, while trade press dates the ownership change to early 2001 — date conflict flagged.
  8. Caloi’s price was never disclosed in dollars — Dorel said only “a high single-digit multiple of EBITDA.” The ~US$85 million figure is unverified.
  9. The FY2020 US$43.125 million goodwill impairment was Dorel Juvenile, not Sports. The Sports impairment was US$237.2 million in Q4 2018.

Files 08–09 — Caterpillar, Varsity Brands

Corrections, coverage notes and filing index

PART II — VARSITY BRANDS / BSN SPORTS / HERFF JONES

Correction to the working thesis before the chain begins. The commonly repeated version of this history is wrong in three places, and the primary sources establish the following: 1. The 2003 take-private of Varsity Brands, Inc. was led by Leonard Green & Partners (Green Equity Investors IV, L.P.) at $6.57/share / $75.8m — not Chartwell/Lincolnshire, and not $131m. Lincolnshire Management owned the separate Riddell football business, which it sold to Fenway Partners in July 2003. 2. Charlesbank did not buy Varsity Brands in 2011. In 2011 the buyer was Herff Jones, Inc., a 100% employee-owned (ESOP) company, which bought VBR Holding Corporation from Leonard Green. Charlesbank (with Partners Group) bought the combined group in 2014. 3. Sport Supply Group was taken private by ONCAP at $13.55/share, not $8.50.
