Stanley Black & Decker & Securitas

Stanley Black & Decker, Inc. · NYSE: SWK · Securitas AB · Niscayah Group AB

Stanley outbid Securitas for Niscayah in 2011 with cash against paper and won. Eleven years later it sold the entire security business to Securitas for $3.2bn. The same two parties, the same assets, the opposite direction, and a $1.2bn book gain that says nothing about whether the original thesis worked.

The Stanley Works / Black & Decker Merger of Equals (context)

All-stock merger of equals

Announced / closed

Announced 2 November 2009; closed 12 March 2010

Headline value

~US$4.5bn all-stock; combined company ~US$8.4bn revenue

Parties

The Stanley Works (NYSE: SWK) / The Black & Decker Corporation (NYSE: BDK) → Stanley Black & Decker, Inc.

Structure

  • Each Black & Decker share converted into 1.275 Stanley Works shares.
  • Stanley Works shareholders held ~50.5% of the combined company — the near-even split that made this a genuine merger of equals rather than an acquisition.
  • Black & Decker shareholder approval obtained 12 March 2010; renamed Stanley Black & Decker, Inc., NYSE: SWK.
  • Created a diversified industrial with hand tools, power tools and accessories, mechanical access solutions and electronic security solutions, and engineered fastening — the security businesses divested in 2022 trace to both legacy companies plus Niscayah.

Financing

All-stock; no external financing.

Advisers

Not disclosed in the completion release.

Key people

  • John F. Lundgren — President & CEO of the combined company
  • Nolan D. Archibald — Chairman/President & CEO of Black & Decker; became Executive Chairman of the combined company [role not stated in the completion release reviewed]

Regulatory and litigation

HSR and international competition clearances obtained pre-closing.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Produced the platform whose non-core segments were systematically divested from 2017 onward (mechanical security majority sale 2017; CSS, MAS and Oil & Gas in 2022), leaving Tools & Outdoor plus Industrial.

The Stanley Works — Acquisition of Niscayah Group AB

Cross-border competitive public tender offer (Swedish takeover rules); contested auction against a strategic rival

Announced / closed

Securitas offer announced ~13 June 2011; Stanley Black & Decker counter-offer announced 27 June 2011; offer document 22 July 2011; acceptance period 25 July – 1 September 2011; declared unconditional and settled September 2011

Headline value

SEK 18.00 per share, ~SEK 6.5bn total (~US$1.2bn)

Parties

Stanley Black & Decker, Inc. (NYSE: SWK), via SBD Holding (Swedish bidco) / Niscayah Group AB (NASDAQ OMX Stockholm) / Securitas AB (competing bidder)

Structure

  • Correction to the commonly cited account: the winning price was SEK 18.00, not SEK 20.00. Securitas’ offer was an all-share exchange offer, not a SEK 18 cash bid.
  • Securitas’ offer (June 2011): 1 new Securitas class A share for every 4.19 Niscayah shares (same ratio for class A and class B), plus SEK 0.05 cash per warrant. Total value ~SEK 5.8bn, a 34% premium to the 3-month VWAP ending 13 May 2011. Acceptance period 20 June – 18 July 2011. Securitas projected ~SEK 200m annual cost synergies, ~SEK 250m restructuring costs across 2011–12, and EPS accretion from 2013. Securitas convened an EGM to approve the share issuance.
  • Stanley’s counter (27 June 2011): SEK 18.00 cash per class A and class B share, SEK 0.05 per warrant — a ~23.8% premium to the value of Securitas’ all-share offer using Securitas’ SEK 60.90 close on 23 June 2011. Cash certainty was the decisive differentiator against paper.
  • Conditions: acceptance by more than 90% of shares on a fully diluted basis; all class A holders to accept and waive pre-emption rights; competition clearances; no MAC; no superior competing offer.
  • Irrevocables: Triton III (Nimble) S.à r.l. and Noonday Asset Management LLP, together holding 19.5% of shares / 13.7% of votes, committed irrevocably — subject to a carve-out permitting them to accept a competing offer at a 7.5%+ premium.
  • Market purchases: on 9 August 2011 Stanley’s Swedish subsidiary disclosed open-market purchases of 72,005,890 class B shares (~19.8% of shares, 13.9% of votes) at prices not exceeding SEK 18 — running the market bid alongside the tender to lock up the stake.
  • Outcome of acceptance period: 74.8% tendered by 1 September 2011; combined with market purchases, ~94.9% of shares and ~96.4% of votes on a fully diluted basis. All conditions satisfied; offer declared unconditional. Extended acceptance period to 23 September 2011. Settlement 9 September 2011 (early acceptors) and ~30 September 2011 (extended period). Compulsory acquisition (squeeze-out) initiated for the residual and the stock delisted from NASDAQ OMX Stockholm.
  • Securitas withdrew rather than raise; it would return for these assets a decade later by buying the Stanley Security business itself.

Financing

Not subject to a financing condition. Funded from existing cash — US$1.9bn available as of 4 June 2011 — plus a US$1.25bn credit facility from Barclays Capital and J.P. Morgan. SBD credit ratings A / Baa1 at the time.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

Niscayah was folded into SBD’s Security segment. That segment subsequently underperformed; SBD recorded $168.4m of asset impairment charges in connection with the 2022 Security divestitures (per the FY2022 10-K divestitures note), a substantial portion economically traceable to the Niscayah-era build-out.

Advisers

  • Stanley Black & Decker: Barclays Capital, J.P. Morgan (financial); Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton LLP and Gernandt & Danielsson Advokatbyrå KB (legal)
  • Niscayah independent committee: UBS (fairness opinion)

Key people

  • John F. Lundgren — President & CEO, Stanley Black & Decker — bidder principal
  • Tomas Franzén — member, Niscayah independent board committee — recommended the Stanley offer
  • Alf Göransson — President & CEO, Securitas AB — losing bidder; presented the acquisition case to the Securitas EGM

Regulatory and litigation

EU and national competition clearances; conducted under the NASDAQ OMX Stockholm Takeover Rules and the Swedish Takeover Act, with Swedish squeeze-out proceedings for the minority. No litigation.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Stanley won the asset but the strategic logic did not hold. The combined Security segment never achieved the margins of Tools, and SBD exited the entire commercial electronic security business in 2022 — selling it to Securitas, the very bidder it had outbid in 2011.

§ 04

Stanley Black & Decker — Sale of Security Business to Securitas AB

Corporate carve-out / divestiture; cross-border sale of a US-headquartered business to a Swedish listed acquirer, funded by a rights issue

Announced / closed

Announced 8 December 2021; closed 22 July 2022

Headline value

US$3.2bn cash, debt-free and cash-free. ~16x trailing adjusted EBITDA (SBD basis, pre-synergy); ~13x 2021E adjusted EBITDA including US$50m of cost synergies (Securitas basis)

Parties

Stanley Black & Decker, Inc. (NYSE: SWK) — seller / Securitas AB (STO: SECU B) — buyer

Structure

  • Sale of the Convergent Security Solutions (CSS) business — the commercial electronic security and healthcare security business lines.
  • Excluded from the Securitas transaction: Stanley Access Technologies, the automatic doors business (Mechanical Access Solutions, MAS). This answers the commonly cited account’s open question — SBD did not retain it either. MAS was sold separately to Allegion plc for $900m cash (announced 22 April 2022, closed 5 July 2022), on 2021 revenue of ~$340m with mid-teens adjusted EBITDA margins. SBD’s FY2022 10-K records MAS net proceeds of $922.2m.
  • A third disposal, the Oil & Gas business, closed 19 August 2022.
  • CSS 2021 revenue: ~$1.7bn forecast at announcement; ~$1.6bn as stated at closing; adjusted EBITDA margin close to 12% before synergies, ~40% recurring revenue.
  • Both CSS and MAS were presented as discontinued operations in SBD’s FY2022 financial statements.
  • Part of SBD’s portfolio reshaping to a focused Tools & Outdoor + Industrial company.

Financing (buyer side)

  • Bridge facility underwritten by SEB, drawn at closing.
  • Rights issue: MSEK 9,583 (~US$915m) before transaction costs, at SEK 46.00 per share, on a 4 new shares for every 7 existing basis (same ratio for class A and class B). Record date 20 Sept 2022; subscription 22 Sept – 11 Oct 2022; outcome announced 14 Oct 2022. ~99.0% subscribed with rights and fully subscribed overall. Proceeds applied to repay part of the bridge.
  • Pre-committed shareholder support at announcement: 44.6% subscription coverage, 42.4% voting support.
  • Balance of the bridge refinanced through long-term bond issuance.

Purchase price allocation / goodwill / impairments (SBD, FY2022 10-K)

  • Pre-tax gain on sale of discontinued operations: $1,197.4m, comprising CSS ~$588.0m and MAS ~$609.0m.
  • CSS net sales in discontinued operations: 2022 $1,056.3m (part-year to 22 July), 2021 $1,971.4m, 2020 $1,784.7m.
  • CSS pre-tax earnings from discontinued operations: 2022 $1,210.9m (including the $1,197.4m gain, i.e. ~$13.5m operating), 2021 $124.3m, 2020 $83.3m.
  • Asset impairment charges of $168.4m recorded in connection with the divestitures.
  • Net proceeds: CSS ~$3.1bn; MAS $922.2m; Oil & Gas — $8.6m loss on sale.
  • Continuing-operations divestiture losses: 2022 $(8.4)m, 2021 $(0.6)m, 2020 $(13.5)m.

Advisers

  • Securitas: Morgan Stanley & Co. International Plc (M&A); SEB (financial adviser and bridge underwriter); K&L Gates and Mannheimer Swartling Advokatbyrå (legal). Neither Goldman Sachs nor Citi advised Securitas — the commonly cited account’s assumption is incorrect.
  • Stanley Black & Decker: not disclosed in the transaction press releases.

Key people

  • James M. Loree — CEO, Stanley Black & Decker (at announcement) — seller principal; also fronted the Allegion sale
  • Donald Allan, Jr. — President & CFO at announcement; President & CEO by closing — “another major milestone in creating a more focused Stanley Black & Decker”
  • Magnus Ahlqvist — President & CEO, Securitas AB — “This transformational acquisition makes us a strong provider of tech-enabled security solutions”
  • Andreas Lindback — CFO, Securitas AB — rights issue and bridge refinancing

Regulatory and litigation

Multi-jurisdictional competition clearances. Securitas announced on 14 July 2022 that it had been granted all required regulatory approvals; specific agencies were not enumerated in the release. Closing followed on 22 July 2022. No litigation.

Outcome and post-deal performance

  • SBD use of proceeds: ~$3bn of net proceeds earmarked at announcement toward a $4bn share repurchase programme ($2.0–2.5bn in Q1 2022, remainder in summer 2022); a $2.3bn buyback was completed in Q1 2022, with the remainder of proceeds directed to debt reduction. Combined with the Allegion and Oil & Gas disposals, SBD raised roughly $4.0bn+ of gross divestiture proceeds in 2022 — into a year of severe tool-market destocking and margin compression.
  • Securitas: created a combined electronic security business of ~US$3.0bn in sales within a ~US$4.2bn group technology/solutions footprint, addressing a ~$70bn electronic security market with access to 500,000+ existing and potential clients. Targeted US$50m of cost synergies fully realized within three years, immediate operating-margin accretion, EPS accretion in the first full year post-completion, and ~50% of future profit contribution from higher-margin technology and solutions. New group financial targets and strategy were presented 24 August 2022.
  • Strategic symmetry: Securitas acquired in 2022 the electronic security platform it had failed to win in 2011, at a far higher price than its Niscayah bid — while Stanley exited the category entirely.
