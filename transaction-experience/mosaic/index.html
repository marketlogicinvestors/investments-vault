A company created by contributing one private giant’s division into a shell that simultaneously acquired a listed competitor, then separated from its 64% parent seven years later through a Section 355 split-off and debt exchange rather than a sale. Three different structural answers to three different problems.

IMC Global — Predecessor History: IMC-Agrico / IMC Phosphates JV and Phosphate Resource Partners

50/50-governed operating joint venture formed by contribution of competing phosphate businesses; later roll-up of the public MLP

Announced / closed

JV formed July 1993; Freeport-McMoRan Inc. merged into IMC December 1997; PLP roll-up completed as part of the 2004 Mosaic combination

Headline value

Not a cash transaction — contribution of businesses. IMC’s 1997 acquisition of Freeport-McMoRan (FTX) was an ~US$800 million stock exchange.

Parties

  • IMC Global Inc. (successor to IMC Fertilizer Group, spun out of International Minerals & Chemical Corporation in 1988; parent renamed Imcera Group, later Mallinckrodt)
  • Freeport-McMoRan Resource Partners, Limited Partnership (FRP), a Delaware MLP, later renamed Phosphate Resource Partners Limited Partnership (PLP), CIK 0000793421
  • Freeport-McMoRan Inc. (FTX) — FRP’s parent and administrative managing general partner until 1997

Structure

  • “IMC Phosphates was formed as a joint venture partnership in July 1993 when PLP and IMC contributed their respective phosphate crop nutrients businesses to IMC Phosphates.” Marketed as IMC-Agrico.
  • Fixed ownership: IMC 58.5% / PLP 41.5% of IMC Phosphates Company.
  • Managing general partner: IMC Phosphates MP Inc. (“MP Co.”), owned 50/50 by IMC and PLP; but “IMC has, through MP Co., the sole authority to make certain decisions affecting IMC Phosphates” — i.e., economic split 58.5/41.5, governance vehicle 50/50 with IMC control.
  • December 1997: Freeport-McMoRan Inc. merged into IMC, making IMC the administrative managing general partner of PLP and owner of FTX’s 51.6% interest in PLP.
  • October 2003: IMC transferred its direct PLP partnership interests to a new wholly owned subsidiary, PRP-GP LLC (“PRP”), which became administrative managing general partner holding 51.58% of PLP units.
  • PLP units at December 31, 2003: public unitholders 50,080,645 (48.4%); IMC-controlled entities 53,385,133 (51.6%); total 103,465,778.
  • 2004: each PLP unit converted into 0.2 IMC/Mosaic shares, eliminating the MLP.

Financing

N/A (contribution/stock).

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

IMC’s own acquisition history — Vigoro Corp. (~US$1.16 billion, 1996, prompting the “IMC Global” name), Western Ag-Minerals Co. (~US$53 million, 1997), Harris Chemical Group (~US$1.4 billion combined consideration, 1998), Freeport-McMoRan Inc. (~US$800 million stock, 1997). Detailed PPAs are in the corresponding IMC filings under CIK 0000820626. [not individually retrieved]

Advisers

Not retrieved for the 1993 JV formation. unverified

Key people

Wendell Bueche and Douglas A. Pertz — successive IMC Global CEOs; Pertz was Chairman & CEO at the time of the Mosaic combination.

Regulatory and litigation

The 1993 JV required antitrust clearance; the combined entity controlled “nearly half of all United States phosphate production.”

Outcome and post-deal performance

IMC Phosphates/IMC-Agrico became the phosphate core of what is now Mosaic’s Phosphates segment. IMC Global’s 2003 revenues were US$2.2 billion at the time of the Cargill announcement.

§ 03

The Mosaic Company — Formation: Combination of Cargill Crop Nutrition and IMC Global Inc. (2004)

Reverse merger / corporate carve-out into a public shell — Cargill contributed its fertilizer businesses to a newly formed holding company that simultaneously acquired IMC Global by merger; Cargill Crop Nutrition was the accounting acquirer, IMC Global the acquired entity

Announced / closed

Merger and Contribution Agreement dated January 26, 2004, announced January 27, 2004; Amendment No. 1 June 15, 2004; Amendment No. 2 October 18, 2004; completed October 22, 2004; Mosaic common stock began trading on the NYSE (MOS) October 25, 2004

Headline value

Purchase price of IMC Global for accounting purposes US$1,679.0 million; combined entity with pro forma trailing revenues ~US$4.1 billion, combined assets ~US$5.4 billion, total debt ~US$2.2 billion, trailing EBITDA before synergies ~US$440 million

Parties

  • Global Nutrition Solutions, Inc. (“Newco”) — the new Delaware holding company, later renamed The Mosaic Company; SEC registrant CIK 0001285785 (filed the S-4 under the Global Nutrition Solutions name)
  • GNS Acquisition Corp. — Newco’s wholly owned merger subsidiary
  • IMC Global Inc. (NYSE: IGL), CIK 0000820626 — surviving the merger and renamed Mosaic Global Holdings Inc.
  • Cargill Contributing Corporations: Cargill, Incorporated; Cargill Fertilizer, Inc.; and GNS I (U.S.) Corp.
  • Phosphate Resource Partners Limited Partnership (PLP), CIK 0000793421 — separate concurrent roll-up merger

Structure

  • Contribution step: immediately before the merger, the Cargill Contributing Corporations contributed the equity interests in the entities owning Cargill’s fertilizer businesses (phosphate, feed phosphates, nitrogen production, worldwide distribution) to Newco in exchange for Newco stock.
  • Merger step: GNS Acquisition Corp. merged with and into IMC Global; IMC survived as a Newco subsidiary and was renamed Mosaic Global Holdings Inc.
  • Exchange ratio: IMC common stockholders received one share of Mosaic common stock for each IMC common share; IMC preferred holders received one share of Mosaic 7.50% mandatory convertible preferred stock per share held (2,750,000 preferred shares, all publicly held post-close).
  • Shares issued to Cargill entities at closing (Form 8-K, Oct 28, 2004):
    • Cargill, Incorporated — 10,810,333 common
    • Cargill Fertilizer, Inc. — 210,569,242 common and 5,458,955 Class B common
    • GNS I (U.S.) Corp. — 29,202,747 common
    • Total: 250,582,322 common + 5,458,955 Class B = 256,041,277 shares
    • Note: the commonly cited “175.3 million shares to Cargill” is not supported by the closing 8-K and appears incorrect.
  • Resulting ownership: Cargill 66.5% of common stock plus 100% of Class B; former IMC common holders 33.5%.
  • Concurrent PLP merger: each PLP unit converted into 0.2 shares of IMC common stock (and, once the Cargill combination closed, into 0.2 shares of Mosaic). PLP was approximately 51.58% owned by IMC through PRP-GP LLC; the special committee obtained a fairness opinion from Houlihan Lokey Howard & Zukin. Board approval of the PLP merger was announced March 1, 2004, and it was expressly not conditioned on the Cargill combination closing.
  • Governance: 11-member board, majority independent — seven directors appointed by Cargill including the chair, four by IMC. Cargill signed an Investor Rights Agreement (amended and restated October 22, 2004) and a Master Transition Services Agreement with Mosaic.
  • Debt consent solicitation: a November 16, 2004 prospectus solicited consents from holders of ~US$1.8 billion of debt securities of Mosaic Global Holdings Inc. (formerly IMC Global) and Phosphate Acquisition Partners L.P.

Financing

  • No cash consideration: this was a stock-for-stock combination plus contribution. Cargill received stock; IMC holders received stock.
  • IMC was highly leveraged and Mosaic assumed US$2,383.7 million of long-term debt through the merger; combined total debt ~US$2.2 billion at announcement. Interest expense rose to US$120.6 million in fiscal 2005 from US$29.2 million in fiscal 2004 as a result.

Cargill's Split-Off of The Mosaic Company (2011)

Tax-free split-off and debt-for-equity exchange under IRC § 355 (with § 368 recapitalization/merger), plus registered secondary "formation offerings" — a controlled-orderly-distribution structure, not a Reverse Morris Trust

Announced / closed

Announced January 18, 2011; Mosaic stockholder approval May 11, 2011; completed May 25, 2011

Headline value

Cargill’s ~64% stake, approximately 285.8–286 million Mosaic shares, reported at approximately US$24.3 billion

Parties

  • Cargill, Incorporated (private, Minnetonka MN) — distributing shareholder
  • The Mosaic Company (NYSE: MOS)
  • GNS II (U.S.) Corp. — the new holding company that became “The Mosaic Company”; SEC registrant CIK 0001511357 for the proxy statement/prospectus (Reg. No. 333-172076); the pre-transaction Mosaic became MOS Holdings Inc.
  • The MAC Trusts (Margaret A. Cargill Philanthropies / charitable trusts) — Cargill stockholders that agreed by exchange agreement to participate fully in the split-off and to sell their Mosaic shares through registered offerings
  • Participating Cargill debt holders in the debt exchanges

Structure

  1. Recapitalization merger: GNS II (U.S.) Corp., a Mosaic subsidiary, merged with Mosaic and became the parent, renamed “The Mosaic Company”; old Mosaic became MOS Holdings Inc. Mosaic’s stock was recapitalized into three classes:

    • Common Stock — one vote per share, publicly traded
    • Class A Common Stock — one vote per share, transfer-restricted, convertible
    • Class B Common Stock — ten votes per share for director elections, one vote otherwise, transfer-restricted

  2. Conversion of Cargill’s 286 million shares into approximately 115 million Common, 60 million Class A, and 111 million Class B.

  3. Split-off: Cargill exchanged approximately 178.3 million Mosaic shares (announced as ~179 million) with participating Cargill stockholders in return for their Cargill common stock.

  4. Debt exchanges: Cargill exchanged approximately 107 million Mosaic shares with holders of Cargill debt.

  5. Formation offerings: an aggregate of 115.0 million shares of Common Stock were sold by selling stockholders in the initial registered offering, with Mosaic committed to register and sell an additional 42.0 million shares within 15 months — at least 157 million shares registered for resale in total.

  6. Lock-up / orderly disposition: approximately 129.0 million shares carried transfer restrictions, released in three equal annual installments beginning on the two-and-a-half-year anniversary of the split-off, with additional annual registered secondary offerings available thereafter.

  7. Governance: significant stockholders (5%+ voting power) required to sign governance agreements with standstill and voting restrictions. No immediate board changes contemplated.

  8. Share repurchase: Mosaic concurrently agreed to repurchase shares from Cargill (announced separately).

Financing

No cash consideration from Mosaic; the transaction was effected in Cargill/Mosaic stock and Cargill debt. Mosaic funded its concurrent repurchase from the balance sheet.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

None — this was a shareholder-level distribution, not a business combination; no purchase accounting at Mosaic.

Advisers

  • Cravath, Swaine & Moore LLP publicly records a role on “Mosaic’s Public Offering of Common Stock and Spin-Off from Cargill”
  • Innisfree M&A Incorporated — proxy solicitor
  • Investment bank roles are not named inside the proxy statement/prospectus excerpts reviewed. unverified

Key people

  • Jim Prokopanko — President & CEO, The Mosaic Company — “This vote of confidence supports our belief that becoming a fully independent company will be good for our stockholders, customers and employees”
  • Greg Page — Chairman & CEO, Cargill (period) [unverified — not named in sources reviewed]

Regulatory and litigation

  • IRS private letter ruling obtained: the merger, split-off and debt exchange were expected to be tax-free to Cargill, Mosaic and their respective shareholders. The § 355 control requirement was satisfied because Cargill transferred shares representing approximately 81% of the voting power for board elections.
  • Minority shareholder approval: approved May 11, 2011 — approximately 58% of non-Cargill shares voted, and over 98% of those voting supported the transaction.
  • Antitrust/other regulatory clearances required. No litigation identified.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Mosaic became a fully independent, widely held public company with no controlling shareholder. Share counts at July 15, 2011: Common 275,825,158; Class A 57,768,374; Class B 112,991,398 (all US$0.01 par).

Vale Fertilizantes S.A. — Acquisition by The Mosaic Company from Vale S.A. (2016–18)

Corporate carve-out from a strategic seller, cash-plus-stock, with the seller taking a minority equity stake and board representation; cross-border (Brazil/Peru/Canada assets)

Announced / closed

Definitive stock purchase agreement announced December 19–20, 2016; terms modified January 2, 2018; closed January 8, 2018

Headline value
  • Announced: US$2.5 billion base consideration (US$1.25 billion cash funded from debt proceeds + ~42.3 million Mosaic shares ≈ 11% of Mosaic), plus an earnout of up to US$260 million over two years tied to financial performance
  • Modified/final: US$1.15 billion cash + 34.2 million Mosaic shares; actual closing consideration US$1.08 billion cash (after working capital and other adjustments) and 34,176,574 shares valued at US$26.92 per share ≈ US$920 million → ~US$2.0 billion aggregate

Parties

  • Buyer: The Mosaic Company (NYSE: MOS), CIK 0001285785
  • Sellers: Vale S.A., Vale Fertilizer Netherlands B.V. and certain affiliates
  • Target: Vale Fertilizantes S.A., renamed Mosaic Fertilizantes (segment) / Mosaic Fertilizantes P&K S.A. (entity)

Structure

  • Stock purchase agreement for the equity of Vale Fertilizantes S.A. and related entities.
  • Assets acquired: five Brazilian phosphate rock mines; four chemical plants; one Brazilian potash mine; an additional 40% economic interest in the Miski Mayo Mine (Peru), raising Mosaic’s aggregate interest to 75%; and the Kronau potash project, Saskatchewan. Capacity: ~4.8 million tonnes of phosphate nutrients and ~500,000 tonnes of potash annually; ~8,000 employees added (Mosaic global headcount to ~17,000).
  • Modification (January 2, 2018): consideration reduced; Vale retained equity ownership in the TIPLAM port facility, with Mosaic keeping usage rights under commercial agreements between the parties. The US$260 million contingent consideration liability recorded at December 31, 2017 was eliminated in the restructured terms.
  • Vale’s stake: minority shareholder with the right to appoint up to two directors; transfer restrictions on the shares for two years post-closing, with customary registration rights thereafter. Luciano Siani Pires (Vale CFO since 2012) was elected to Mosaic’s board at closing.
  • New reporting segment “Mosaic Fertilizantes” created; Miski Mayo results consolidated into the Phosphates segment.
  • Brazil Landco: 49% Mosaic ownership with 51% non-controlling interest.

Financing

Cash portion funded from debt proceeds (US$1.25 billion contemplated at announcement).

Regulatory and litigation

Brazilian (CADE) and other competition/regulatory approvals; the ~13-month gap between signing and closing reflects that process and the renegotiation. No transaction litigation identified.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Mosaic became Brazil’s leading fertilizer producer with a dedicated Mosaic Fertilizantes segment. Announced target: >US$80 million of after-tax synergies and earnings accretion by 2018. Vale became a ~11% (pre-reduction; ~9% post-reduction, unverified) shareholder of Mosaic.
