A complete corporate life cycle in eight transactions: a distressed bargain, a leveraged transformational acquisition written off within four years, a reverse-merger pivot, the sale of the original business for a third of its stated price, and a $100m facility that foreclosed. § 01

Transaction map

Every transaction on this file, in order

Navarre bought Encore out of bankruptcy for ~$10m in 2002 and FUNimation for $117.8m in 2005 on a $165m GE Capital facility. It impaired $73.4m of goodwill in one quarter of FY2009, sold FUNimation for $24.0m in 2011, reverse-merged with SpeedFC in 2012, sold the legacy distribution business for a stated $15m that realised ~$5m, levered $100m for Fifth Gear, and was foreclosed on in June 2016.

§ 02

Navarre Corporation — Acquisition of Encore Software, Inc. (Section 363 bankruptcy asset sale)

Distressed asset sale / §363 bankruptcy purchase (public-company bolt-on, all-cash + assumed debt)

Announced / closed

Bankruptcy court approval and closing July 31, 2002 (8-K event date); press release August 5, 2002; 8-K filed August 14, 2002; 8-K/A with audited target financials and pro formas filed December 4, 2002

Headline value

~$9.75m–$10m all-in ($7.0m cash + up to $2.75m of notes/assumed debt); pro forma exhibit describes “the approximately $10 million purchase price for Encore”

Parties

  • Buyer: Encore Acquisition Corporation, a wholly-owned subsidiary of Navarre Corporation (NASDAQ: NAVR)
  • Seller: Encore Software, Inc. (debtor-in-possession), via the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Central District of California (Chapter 11 filed March 22, 2002)

Structure

  • $7.0m cash at closing
  • $650,000 contingent promissory note, payable subject to an approval condition by October 17, 2002
  • $500,000 unsecured promissory note due January 1, 2003
  • Assumption of ~$1.6m of Encore bank debt owed to Comerica Bank
  • Assets acquired: fixed assets, intellectual property, inventory, receivables, contract rights; Encore’s California operations retained
  • No Navarre shares issued — press release states the transaction caused “no share dilution for Navarre” and no assumption of Encore’s general liabilities. There was no stock-plus-earnout structure [corrects the “$2.5m cash + stock earnout” premise — not supported by any Navarre filing]
  • Exhibits 10.1–10.4 to the 8-K are the purchase agreements and the two promissory notes; Exhibit 99.1 is the August 5, 2002 press release

Financing

Funded from Navarre’s existing cash and revolving credit facility; no acquisition-specific facility disclosed in the 8-K.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

  • 8-K/A (Dec 4, 2002) pro formas state the ~$10m purchase price “has been allocated on a preliminary basis using information currently available”; allocation “expected to be finalized by the end of fiscal 2003.” No line-item split of tangible assets / intangibles / goodwill was published in the pro forma exhibit.
  • Encore historical (per 8-K/A pro forma): FY2001 net sales $32.047m, net loss $(11.960)m; four months ended April 30, 2002 net sales $7.627m, net loss $(4.736)m
  • Encore goodwill was later folded into Navarre’s publishing segment, whose entire goodwill balance was written down in FY2009 (see FUNimation impairment section — publishing goodwill fell from $81.697m to $8.285m in the quarter ended Sept 30, 2008)

Advisers

None disclosed in the 8-K or press release.

Key people

  • Eric H. Paulson — Navarre Chairman, President & CEO — principal, quoted on strategy of augmenting “core distribution operations in key entertainment segments with both acquired and owned content”
  • Jim Gilbertson — Navarre VP & CFO — deal contact
  • Joyce Fleck — Navarre VP Marketing
  • Mike Bell — Encore Software CEO and founder — retained to run the acquired business
  • Correction Cary Deacon was not involved at Encore; he became Navarre’s CEO later (in office by 2007–2011). Ron Chaimowitz (Genius Products) has no role disclosed in any Navarre filing regarding Encore. [unverified / not found in primary sources]

Regulatory and litigation

U.S. Bankruptcy Court, C.D. Cal. approval of the §363 sale; no antitrust filing disclosed.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Encore Software, Inc. remained a Navarre subsidiary for twelve years and became the core of Navarre’s software publishing business. It was divested July 9, 2014 as part of the WYNIT transaction — WD Encore Software, LLC was one of the six WYNIT purchaser entities and Encore Software, Inc. was one of the eight selling entities (see WYNIT deal below). Encore was never sold to a separate buyer.

Navarre Corporation — Acquisition of FUNimation Productions, Ltd. and The FUNimation Store, Ltd.

Public-company transformational acquisition; purchase of 100% of general and limited partnership interests; cash + stock + earnout, leveraged with a new $165m credit facility

Announced / closed

Purchase agreement signed January 10, 2005 (8-K filed January 11, 2005); closed May 11, 2005 (8-K filed May 17, 2005); 8-K/A with audited FUNimation financials and pro formas filed July 25, 2005

Headline value

~$117.8m recorded purchase price — $100.4m cash + ~$14.1m stock + $3.3m transaction costs, plus up to $17.0m contingent cash over five years

Parties

  • Buyers: Navarre CP, LLC; Navarre CS, LLC; Navarre CLP, LLC — three wholly-owned Navarre subsidiaries
  • Targets: FUNimation Productions, Ltd. and The FUNimation Store, Ltd. (Texas limited partnerships)
  • Sellers: the FUNimation partners, principally Gen Fukunaga and affiliates

Structure

  • Cash: $100,400,000 at closing, “plus potential adjustments up to $5 million”
  • Stock: shares equal to $25.0m divided by the Closing Share Price, with a collar: not fewer than 1,495,216 and not more than 1,827,486 shares. The maximum, 1,827,486 unregistered Navarre common shares, was issued at closing (share price collapsed into the low end of the collar, so the collar cap bound); the stock was recorded at a fair value of only $14.145m, not $25m
  • Shares issued unregistered under a private placement, subject to a Registration Rights Agreement (Exhibit 10.1(e) form)
  • Earnout: $17,000,000 of cash payable over five years contingent on attainment of predetermined operating targets
  • Indemnification: $20,000,000 cap with a $1,000,000 deductible
  • Termination fee: $2,500,000 payable by either party in specified circumstances; outside termination date May 15, 2005
  • Ancillary agreements filed as forms 10.1(a)–(f): Assignment and Assumption, Employment Agreement, Escrow Agreement, Non-Competition Agreement, Registration Rights Agreement, Release
  • Closing conditions: Hart-Scott-Rodino clearance, accuracy of reps, execution of employment agreements, minimum cash condition
  • Gen Fukunaga employment: five-year term as President & CEO of FUNimation Productions, Ltd.; base salary $350,000; option on 150,000 shares at $8.35, three-year vest; performance bonuses of up to $9.0m in aggregate tied to EBIT targets through 2010; severance of two years’ salary plus bonus on termination without cause

Financing

  • $165,000,000 senior secured credit facility with General Electric Capital Corporation / GE Commercial Finance, replacing Navarre’s existing lines
  • Term Loan B: $140,000,000, six-year maturity, fully drawn at closing
  • Revolving sub-facility: $25,000,000, five-year maturity
  • Initial interest rate 8%, adjustable per the agreement
  • Security: first-priority lien on substantially all assets
  • Documented as the Second Amended and Restated Credit Agreement (Exhibit 10.2 to the May 17, 2005 8-K)
  • $2,917,000 of debt issuance costs capitalized, amortized over six years
  • Correction No Bank of America / LaSalle facility and no subordinated notes were used for this acquisition — the acquisition financing was the GE Capital facility. Navarre separately completed a $19.95m private placement on March 21, 2006 (Craig-Hallum Capital Group sole placement agent) to retire its highest-rate debt.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

  • Preliminary allocation, 8-K/A filed July 25, 2005 (Exhibit 99.4 pro formas):
    • Cash paid $100.400m + common stock issued $14.145m + transaction costs $3.300m = total consideration $117.845m
    • Goodwill: $80.459m (“preliminary amount of purchase price assigned to goodwill”)
    • Identifiable intangible assets: $22.647m (not disaggregated in the pro forma exhibit into content library / licensing agreements / trade names; later 10-K disclosures refer to masters, license relationships and trademarks)
    • Allocation “subject to finalization of a third party valuation”; goodwill could shift up to $5.0m based on final equity value and receivables holdback
    • $17.0m contingent consideration excluded from the initial allocation
  • FY2009 impairment (the write-off): in the quarter ended September 30, 2008 Navarre recorded a $73.412m non-cash goodwill impairment, driven by market capitalization sustained below book value. Publishing-segment goodwill fell from $81.697m to $8.285m; distribution-segment goodwill of $3,000 went to $0. Note 14 to the Q2 FY2009 10-Q: “it was determined that the fair value of the Company’s FUNimation and BCI reporting units was less than their carrying value.” No separate FUNimation-only figure was published; FUNimation was the dominant component.
  • Full fiscal 2009: $111.1m of pre-tax impairment and other charges — publishing segment $110.5m, distribution segment $591,000 — including a further $3.1m goodwill impairment in Q4 FY2009. FY2009 net loss from continuing operations $88.4m, $(2.44)/diluted share. Selected Financial Data in the FY2009 10-K shows goodwill impairment of $(89,452) thousand for FY2009 vs. $0 for FY2008.
  • FY2011: an additional $2.1m of non-cash impairment charges in Q4 FY2011 on FUNimation, plus the loss on sale (below)

Advisers

  • Bear, Stearns & Co. Inc. — financial advisor to Navarre
  • A.G. Edwards & Sons, Inc. — financial advisor to FUNimation
  • Houlihan Lokey — retained May 2010 for the eventual divestiture (see next entry)

Key people

  • Eric H. Paulson — Navarre Chairman, President & CEO — deal principal; “The acquisition of FUNimation is an exciting transaction for everyone involved”
  • Jim Gilbertson — Navarre VP & CFO — deal contact on the press release (763-535-8333)
  • Haug Scharnowski — Navarre VP Corporate Relations — IR contact
  • Gen Fukunaga — FUNimation founder/President, post-closing President & CEO of FUNimation Productions, Ltd. — principal seller and five-year employment agreement counterparty; “We are very excited to join the Navarre family”
  • Cindy Brennan Fukunaga — FUNimation co-founder — not named in the 8-K, purchase agreement exhibits or press release [unverified as a named party]
  • Charles Cheney — described in the commonly cited account as CFO — not named in any Navarre FUNimation deal filing; Navarre’s CFO on this deal was Jim Gilbertson unverified

Regulatory and litigation

Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period expiry was a closing condition. Within months of closing Navarre restated: 10-K/A Amendment No. 2 and 10-Q/As filed November 21, 2005 restating FY2003–FY2005 and Q1–Q3 FY2005 and Q1 FY2006 for (i) FIN 46(R) consolidation of Mix & Burn, Inc. as a variable interest entity — $343,000 FY2004 operating loss and $2.2m FY2005 operating loss; (ii) $388,000 additional expense for the former CFO’s April 2004 separation agreement; (iii) CEO deferred compensation under Eric Paulson’s 2001 employment agreement under APB 12 — $408,000 (FY2003), $288,000 (FY2004), $288,000 (FY2005). A prior June 2005 restatement had already added $1.5m (Q3 FY2004) and $2.2m (Q1 FY2005) of CEO compensation expense and reclassified a $2.4m deferred tax benefit out of Q3 FY2005 income into common stock. The company disclosed material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting and told investors that prior financial statements “should no longer be relied upon.” No SEC inquiry or investigation is disclosed in the 10-K/A. [The brief’s “SEC issues” appear to be the restatement and material weaknesses, not an enforcement action — unverified beyond that.]

Outcome and post-deal performance

FY2006 guidance at announcement was $710–720m net sales and $40–45m EBITDA ($1.26–$1.42/fully diluted share); Navarre missed badly. FUNimation’s own pre-deal results were $63.7m sales / $24.9m pre-tax income (2002), $81.6m / $30.5m (2003), $72.4m / $29.8m (2004). Essentially the entire $80.5m of goodwill was impaired within 3.5 years. FUNimation was sold March 31, 2011 for $24.0m (see next entry) — roughly a $94m destruction of value against the $117.8m recorded purchase price.

Navarre Corporation — Divestiture of FUNimation Entertainment

Divestiture / carve-out sale of partnership interests to a management-led investment group (all-cash)

Announced / closed

Strategic-alternatives review announced May 27, 2010; closed March 31, 2011 with funding April 1, 2011; 8-K filed April 5, 2011; 8-K/A filed April 6, 2011

Headline value

$24,000,000 cash (net proceeds ~$22.5m) — against a $117.8m recorded 2005 purchase price

Parties

  • Sellers: Navarre CS, LLC; Navarre CP, LLC; Navarre CLP, LLC, with Navarre Corporation as guarantor
  • Buyers: FUNimation GP, LLC; Anime LP Holdings, LLC; FUNimation LP, LLC — three Texas LLCs formed by an investment group including Gen Fukunaga, FUNimation’s CEO and a minority stakeholder in the buying group
  • Assets: substantially all assets of FUNimation Productions, Ltd. and animeOnline, Ltd., conveyed via purchase of the partnership interests

Structure

  • $24,000,000 aggregate consideration, “paid via wire transfer on the Funding Date of April 1, 2011” — all cash, no seller note, no escrow or holdback specified in the agreement
  • Indemnification: general cap $5,000,000; basket/threshold $500,000; independent-contractor liability sub-cap $250,000
  • Buyers obligated to seek release of Navarre’s $3.5m property lease guaranty; in exchange Navarre provided a five-year $1.5m letter of credit reducing by $300,000 per year
  • Continuing commercial arrangements: Transition Services Agreement (Navarre Corporation), Distribution Agreement (Navarre Distribution Services, Inc.), Logistics Agreement (Navarre Logistical Services, Inc.) — Navarre became FUNimation’s exclusive U.S. distributor and logistics/fulfillment provider
  • 8-K reported under Items 1.01, 2.01, 2.06 (material impairment), 5.02, 7.01, 9.01

Financing

None on Navarre’s side. Navarre ended fiscal 2011 debt-free before receiving proceeds.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

  • Loss on sale: $8.1m pre-tax / $5.198m after tax, recorded in fiscal 2011 as “Loss gain on sale of discontinued operations, net”
  • $2.1m of non-cash impairment charges in Q4 FY2011 on FUNimation
  • 8-K guided to “approximately $11 million” combined non-cash impairment and pre-tax loss on sale in Q4 FY2011
  • All FUNimation results, assets and liabilities reclassified to discontinued operations for all periods presented in the FY2011 10-K; FY2011 discontinued-ops net loss $1.310m after tax; Q4 FY2011 discontinued-ops loss $849,000 pre-tax / $536,000 after tax
  • Cumulative economic loss: ~$94m of the $117.8m purchase price, split between the FY2009 goodwill write-off (see above) and the FY2011 loss on sale

Advisers

Houlihan Lokey retained May 2010 to assist in structuring a potential sale. No advisor named in the April 2011 closing press release.

Key people

  • Cary L. Deacon — Navarre CEO (through the sale process) — “We ended fiscal 2011 debt free, prior to our receipt of the proceeds from this transaction”
  • Reid Porter — Navarre COO, later Interim CEO — “This transaction ends a comprehensive sales process undertaken over the last year”; as Interim CEO said the Japanese anime business “was not a strategic fit”
  • Gen Fukunaga — FUNimation CEO; minority stakeholder in the buyer group; management buyer

Regulatory and litigation

None disclosed.

Outcome and post-deal performance

  • Correction to the commonly cited account’s timeline: primary sources show no 2006 attempt and no November 2007 announced sale of FUNimation. The Nov 27, 2007 press release was the appointment of Brian Burke as COO; the April 2008 filing was an investor presentation. The only sale process was the one announced May 27, 2010, which nearly failed — the January 31, 2011 Q3 FY2011 release stated: “The company has not yet received an adequate offer in connection with its efforts to sell FUNimation Entertainment. Although this sales process continues, if the company does not receive an adequate offer in the fourth quarter, it anticipates that these efforts will be halted.” A deal closed two months later. [The “failed 2006 attempt” and “Nov 2007 announced sale that fell through” are unverified and appear not to exist in the EDGAR record.]
  • Correction: the 2011 buyer entities were FUNimation GP, LLC / Anime LP Holdings, LLC / FUNimation LP, LLC. “Group 1200 Media” was the name adopted in May 2013, two years later, when Fukunaga consolidated FUNimation’s divisions under a holding-company brand — it was not the 2011 acquirer. [per Anime News Network, May 10, 2013]
  • Navarre FY2011 continuing-ops results: net sales $490.9m (down 1%), net income $11.2m / $0.30 per diluted share; Q4 net sales $124.3m (down 6.6%), net income $4.0m / $0.10 per diluted share
  • Sale proceeds were earmarked to “build or acquire new businesses and services to diversify revenue streams” — this became the SpeedFC acquisition

Navarre Corporation — Acquisition of SpeedFC, Inc.

Reverse-merger-like transformational acquisition (target holders took ~31.4% of the buyer, rising to ~38.5% on full earnout); cash + stock + cash/stock earnout

Announced / closed

Merger agreement September 27, 2012 (8-K filed September 28, 2012); Amendment No. 1 October 29, 2012; shareholder vote and closing November 20, 2012 (8-K filed November 21, 2012); 8-K/A with target financials and pro formas January 25, 2013; Amendment No. 2 June 26, 2013

Headline value

$50,000,000 initial consideration ($25m cash + $25m stock); total recorded consideration including contingent consideration $54,172,000

Parties

  • Acquirer: Navarre Corporation (Minnesota); merger vehicle SFC Acquisition Co., Inc.
  • Target: SpeedFC, Inc. (Delaware) — e-commerce fulfillment, 3PL and call-center provider, Dallas/Richardson TX
  • Equityholders’ representative: Jeffrey B. Zisk, SpeedFC President/CEO and founder (50.1% holder)

Structure

  • Cash at closing: $25,000,000, subject to escrow, holdback and net working capital adjustment
  • Stock at closing: 17,095,186 Navarre common shares, valued at $25,000,000
