Verizon Communications Inc. / Vodafone Group Plc — acquisition of the 45% minority interest in Verizon Wireless

Minority stake buy-in (acquisition of a noncontrolling interest in an already-consolidated subsidiary); cash-and-stock share purchase of a holding company, accounted for as an equity transaction under ASC 810

Announced / closed

Stock Purchase Agreement entered September 2, 2013; announced September 2, 2013 (Verizon 8-K). Verizon and Vodafone shareholder meetings January 28, 2014. Completed February 21, 2014. Vodafone share consolidation and Return of Value took effect February 24, 2014; cash distributed March 4, 2014.

Headline value

~$130bn (per the pro forma: $131,274m of total consideration) — at the time the third-largest corporate transaction ever announced.

Parties

Verizon Communications Inc. (buyer; CIK 0000732712) / Vodafone Group Plc (seller; CIK 0000839923) / Vodafone Americas Finance 1 Inc. (“Holdco”), the acquired entity, which indirectly held Vodafone’s 45% interest in Cellco Partnership d/b/a Verizon Wireless

Structure

  • Verizon acquired all of the issued and outstanding capital stock of Holdco (Vodafone Americas Finance 1 Inc.), which indirectly held, through other US subsidiaries, Vodafone’s indirect 45% interest in Verizon Wireless. A stock purchase rather than an asset purchase — this is the key to the tax outcome.
  • Consideration (as agreed, 8-K of Sept 2013):
    • ~$58.89bn cash
    • ~$60.15bn of Verizon common stock
    • $5.0bn of Verizon senior unsecured notes issued directly to Vodafone (8- and 11-year maturities)
    • $3.5bn — transfer of Verizon’s indirectly owned 23.1% interest in Vodafone Omnitel N.V. to Vodafone
    • ~$2.5bn other consideration
  • Collar on the stock consideration: the number of shares was set on the 20-trading-day VWAP ending on the third business day before closing, with the reference price floored at $47.00 and capped at $51.00. Expected issuance range disclosed in the prospectus: ~1.18bn to ~1.28bn shares.
  • Actual settlement: 20-day VWAP through February 18, 2014 = US$47.1852 (i.e., the floor bound); 1,274,764,121 Verizon common shares issued, valued at $61,342m in the pro forma. Diluted weighted-average shares rose from 2,874m to 4,149m pro forma.
  • Pro forma consideration build: common stock $61,342m + external debt $59,761m + Verizon notes to Vodafone $5,000m + assumption of VAI preferred stock $1,671m + Omnitel credit $3,500m = $131,274m.
  • On completion, Verizon and Vodafone terminated the 1999 U.S. Wireless Alliance Agreement and the 2000 Investment Agreement — the two instruments that had governed the joint venture since its formation.
  • Termination fees payable by Verizon: $1.55bn if Verizon shareholders failed to approve; $4.65bn on a Verizon board recommendation change; $10.0bn for financing failure.
  • Tax: structured as a sale of UK/Dutch-held US holding company shares. Vodafone’s gain fell largely outside UK corporation tax under the UK substantial shareholdings exemption, and the US-side gain was subject to limited US tax because the disposed entity was not a US real property holding corporation; Vodafone reported a US$5.0bn total tax charge on the disposal (versus a ~$130bn transaction) [the SSE mechanism itself is unverified from primary filings; the tax charge is reported in Vodafone’s FY2014 20-F]. Vodafone recognised a pre-tax gain of £45.0bn and total profit for the year of £59.4bn, and recognised deferred tax assets of £19.3bn.
  • Vodafone shareholder Return of Value: ~US$84.0bn (£51.4bn) — comprising US$59.3bn in Verizon shares (all of the Verizon consideration shares were passed through) and US$23.9bn in cash, delivered through a B share (capital option) / C share (income option) structure, followed by a share consolidation of 6 new ordinary shares for every 11 existing (post-consolidation count 28,811,864,298 shares). Per Vodafone ordinary share: ~0.026 Verizon shares plus cash. Vodafone shareholders approved on January 28, 2014 — 68.55% of shares in issue voted, 99.8% in favour.
  • Verizon shareholders approved (i) the share issuance and (ii) a charter amendment increasing authorised common stock, at the special meeting on January 28, 2014.

Terminated in Q4 2013 on effectiveness of a term loan agreement; Verizon expensed $0.2bn of bridge fees.

  • Joint book-running managers on the $49bn: Barclays, BofA Merrill Lynch, J.P. Morgan, Morgan Stanley (the four active books), with Citigroup, Credit Suisse, Mitsubishi UFJ Securities, Mizuho Securities, RBC Capital Markets, RBS and Wells Fargo Securities.

  • February 2014 international offering: €3.0bn and £850m benchmark notes (8-, 12- and 20-year maturities).
  • Total external debt raised for the transaction: ~$66.4bn (including the $5bn of notes issued to Vodafone).

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and accounting

  • No purchase accounting and no goodwill. Verizon already held a controlling 55% interest and consolidated Verizon Wireless, so under ASC Topic 810, “changes in a parent’s ownership interest where the parent retains a controlling financial interest in its subsidiary will be accounted for as equity transactions.” No remeasurement of assets or liabilities occurred; the difference between the consideration and the carrying amount of the noncontrolling interest was recorded within equity.
  • The pro forma eliminated the $55,465m carrying value of Vodafone’s 45% noncontrolling interest, reducing consolidated noncontrolling interests from $56,580m to $1,115m.
  • Verizon total debt: $51.99bn at Dec 31, 2012 ($4,369m current + $47,618m long-term) → $93.59bn at Dec 31, 2013 ($3,933m current + $89,658m long-term), i.e. pre-funded before the February 2014 close; peaked at ~$113.6bn during 2014 unverified before a multi-year deleveraging.
  • 2013 Verizon standalone metrics cited in the February 2014 investor deck: revenue $120.6bn, operating cash flow $38.8bn, free cash flow $22.2bn, adjusted EBITDA $42.1bn. Verizon described the deal as accretive to EPS at close with “no integration risk.”

Regulatory and litigation

  • FCC approval under the Communications Act (transfer of control of licences) — granted without conditions.
  • European Commission approval for the transfer of Verizon’s 23.1% Omnitel interest to Vodafone.
  • UK High Court sanction of the Vodafone scheme of arrangement implementing the B/C share Return of Value and share consolidation.
  • Verizon shareholder approval of the share issuance and the charter amendment (both passed January 28, 2014). No material deal litigation.

Outcome and post-deal performance

  • Verizon obtained 100% of the cash flows of the largest US wireless carrier, eliminating the distributions that had previously leaked to Vodafone ($8.3bn to Vodafone in 2012; $3.15bn in 2013).
  • Vodafone’s own market value roughly halved on completion as the Verizon stake left the group; the residual “Vodafone” was reduced to ~$100bn of value, and the company launched Project Spring, a £19bn network investment programme, with the retained proceeds.
  • Verizon’s leverage peaked at ~3.5x net debt/EBITDA in 2014 and was brought back toward ~2.5x by 2017–2018 through free cash flow, asset sales (the $10.54bn tower sale to American Tower in 2015, the $9.1bn wireline sale to Frontier in 2016) and debt tenders.
  • The $49bn bond remained the largest corporate bond sale on record until 2016 (Anheuser-Busch InBev, $46bn — still smaller) and was surpassed only conceptually; it retained the record through at least 2025 unverified.

Earlier history — formation of Verizon Wireless and the 1999–2006 arrangements

  • September 21, 1999: Bell Atlantic and Vodafone AirTouch Plc agreed to combine their US wireless assets into a new national wireless business — documented in the U.S. Wireless Alliance Agreement (1999), terminated at the 2014 closing.
  • April 3, 2000: the Verizon brand was launched; the joint venture began operating April 4, 2000. Governed also by an Investment Agreement (2000), likewise terminated at the 2014 closing.
  • June 30, 2000: the Bell Atlantic–GTE merger closed, forming Verizon Communications Inc.; GTE Wireless was folded into Verizon Wireless, making it the largest US wireless carrier. Final ownership: Verizon 55% with management control / Vodafone 45% indirect.
  • Put option: Vodafone held a put right to require Verizon to purchase portions of its interest (up to a capped amount, commonly cited at ~$20bn over exercise windows in 2003–2004 and 2006–2007). Vodafone did not exercise; the put lapsed in August 2006. Vodafone sought SEC no-action relief in June 2006 in connection with the arrangements (Linklaters incoming letter, June 8, 2006).
  • 2004–2006 dividend dispute: Verizon Wireless made no distributions to its owners between 2005 and 2011, retaining cash to fund network build-out and to repay intercompany debt — a persistent grievance for Vodafone shareholders, who pressed for either a dividend or an exit and, from 2006, publicly agitated for a sale of the stake.
  • Distributions resumed only in the final years: a $10bn distribution declared in 2011 and paid January 31, 2012 (Vodafone’s share $4.5bn); $8.5bn distributed by year-end 2012 (Vodafone received $8.3bn in 2012 in total per Verizon’s figures, reflecting timing); $7bn distributed in 2013 (Vodafone’s share $3.15bn).
