Pacific Cycle, Cannondale & Dorel

Dorel Industries Inc. · TSX: DII.A / DII.B · Pacific Cycle LLC · Cycling Sports Group

Two distressed brand purchases assembled a mass-market platform that a strategic bought for $310m, then bolted on the premium channel for another $195m. Fourteen years later the segment carried a $237m impairment, a take-private failed at the vote, and the whole thing sold for $810m.

Transaction map

Every transaction on this file, in order

Wind Point’s Pacific Cycle bought Brunswick’s bicycle business for $60m and Schwinn/GT out of bankruptcy for $86m, then sold to Dorel for $310.98m in 2004. Dorel added Cannondale in 2008 and Caloi in 2013. The Cerberus and Koch take-private was terminated the day before the vote in February 2021, and the strategic review it triggered produced the $810m sale of Dorel Sports to Pon Holdings.

Pacific Cycle — Predecessor Transactions (Brunswick bicycle carve-out 2000/01; Schwinn/GT §363 sale 2001)

  1. Corporate carve-out / non-core divestiture; (b) Distressed §363 bankruptcy asset auction

Announced / closed
    1. Brunswick Corporation bicycle business → Pacific Cycle, LLC: Brunswick’s FY2000 Form 10-K states “the sales of the camping and bicycle businesses were completed during 2000.” Trade press reported the change of ownership at the start of 2001. [date conflict — flagged]
    1. Schwinn/GT: bankruptcy auction won by Pacific Cycle, September 2001
Headline value
  1. US$60 million; (b) US$86 million

Parties

    1. Seller: Brunswick Corporation (NYSE: BC, CIK 0000014930), divesting its former Outdoor Recreation segment. Buyer: Pacific Cycle, LLC (Wind Point Partners-backed). Brands: Mongoose, Mongoose Pro, Roadmaster.
    1. Debtor: Schwinn/GT (Schwinn Cycling & Fitness / GT Bicycle Products), then owned by Questor Partners Fund. Winning bidder: Pacific Cycle, outbidding Huffy Corporation.

Structure

  • Wind Point Partners took control of Pacific Cycle in 1998 in partnership with founder Chris Hornung.
  • Brunswick deal was a divisional asset/stock sale out of Brunswick’s discontinued Outdoor Recreation segment (fishing, camping, bicycle, cooler, marine accessories, hunting sports accessories).
  • Schwinn/GT was a distressed sale out of Chapter 11; Pacific Cycle acquired the Schwinn and GT brands and related assets; the Schwinn Fitness business went separately (to Direct Focus). Questor had acquired Schwinn in 1997 and merged it with GT Bicycles.

Financing

Wind Point Partners equity plus acquisition debt. Terms not publicly disclosed. unverified

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

  • Brunswick recorded a US$178.0 million pretax impairment on the bicycle business in 1999, including a US$133.6 million goodwill write-off, citing pricing pressure from Asian imports (Brunswick FY2000 10-K).
  • Brunswick recorded a US$305.3 million pretax / US$229.6 million after-tax loss on disposition of all discontinued businesses in 2000; expected aggregate pretax cash from the dispositions ~US$135 million. Bicycle-specific proceeds and loss are not separately itemized.
  • Pacific Cycle was privately held; no PPA disclosure exists for either acquisition.

Advisers

Lincoln Partners (now Lincoln International) acted as financial advisor to Brunswick Corporation on the bicycle divestiture.

Key people

  • Chris Hornung — founder, Pacific Cycle
  • Rich Kracum — Managing Director, Wind Point Partners
  • George W. Buckley — Chairman & CEO, Brunswick Corporation (quoted on divestiture rationale)

Regulatory and litigation

Schwinn/GT sale required US Bankruptcy Court approval. Separately, CPSC recalls of Mongoose and Roadmaster bicycles were announced with Brunswick Corp. in 2002.

Outcome and post-deal performance

The two acquisitions gave Pacific Cycle a ~27% share of total US bicycle sales and ~44% of the mass-merchant channel, and annual sales >US$325 million by 2003 — the platform Wind Point sold to Dorel in 2004 for US$310 million.

Pacific Cycle LLC — Acquisition by Dorel Industries Inc. from Wind Point Partners (2004)

Private equity exit / sponsor-to-strategic sale; 100% membership-interest purchase; cross-border (US target, Canadian TSX-listed acquirer)

Announced / closed

Announced January 13, 2004 (Form 6-K news release); closed February 3, 2004; completion announced February 4, 2004

Headline value

US$310 million all-cash; final recorded net purchase price US$310,976,000 including acquisition costs; plus up to US$10,423,000 contingent earnout

Parties

  • Buyer: Dorel Industries Inc. (TSX: DII.A / DII.B; then also SEC-registered foreign private issuer, CIK 0000843405)
  • Target: Pacific Cycle, LLC (Madison, Wisconsin; distribution centres in Illinois and California)
  • Seller: investment group led by Wind Point Partners (Chicago / Southfield, MI)

Structure

  • Dorel “acquired all the outstanding shares of Pacific Cycle, LLC” on February 3, 2004 (FY2004 financial statements, Note 3).
  • Consideration: cash at closing funded principally by drawings on Dorel’s revolving credit facilities, plus a balance of sale payable of US$10,556,000.
  • Contingent consideration: management deferred payments tied to three-year earnings targets; “a maximum of $10,423 [thousand] would be recorded as an additional element of purchase price and would increase goodwill” if targets are met; further amounts above the targets are treated as compensation expense, not purchase price. This was the mechanism retaining founder Chris Hornung’s team.
  • Pacific Cycle was operated as a standalone Dorel division; Dorel created a new reporting segment, Recreational/Leisure, for it (later renamed Dorel Sports).
  • Brands acquired: Schwinn, Mongoose, GT, Roadmaster, InStep, Pacific, Murray.

Financing

  • Long-term debt drawn at acquisition: US$288,000,000.
  • Dorel’s unsecured credit facility was increased from US$245.0 million to US$470.0 million effective January 29, 2004 (four days before closing) specifically to fund the deal.
  • Weighted average interest rate on the acquisition debt through 2004: 4.37%.
  • Total long-term debt at December 30, 2004: US$505.816 million non-current / US$513.541 million including current portion.
  • Syndicate member banks are not named in the SEC-filed MD&A or financial statements. [unverified — bank group identities not disclosed in the public filings reviewed]

Purchase price allocation / goodwill / impairments (Dorel FY2004 consolidated financial statements, Note 3, US$000s):

  • Cash 3,734
  • Accounts receivable 32,709
  • Inventories 51,685
  • Capital assets 1,590
  • Trademarks 127,000
  • Other 2,385
  • Goodwill 147,593
  • Total assets acquired 366,696
  • Accounts payable and accrued liabilities (55,720)
  • Net purchase price 310,976
  • Goodwill assigned entirely to the Recreational/Leisure segment; earnout, if earned, would increase goodwill by up to 10,423.
  • Pro forma FY2004 (as if acquired January 1, 2004): net sales US$1,700,596k; net income US$99,123k.
  • Recreational/Leisure segment, FY2004 actual (11 months): revenue US$385.883m; gross profit US$85.752m (22.2%); operating expenses US$35.756m (9.3%); earnings from operations US$49.447m (12.8%).

Advisers

Not disclosed in Dorel’s SEC-filed 6-K, MD&A or annual financial statements. unverified

Key people

  • Martin Schwartz — President & CEO, Dorel Industries — deal principal and public spokesman
  • Jeffrey Schwartz — CFO, Dorel Industries — financing / credit facility upsize
  • Chris Hornung — founder, Pacific Cycle (founded 1977, Madison WI) — retained via earnout/deferred comp
  • Rich Kracum — Managing Director, Wind Point Partners — sponsor lead (per Lincoln International deal record on the earlier Brunswick transaction)
  • Alice Tillett — later President, Pacific Cycle division (from 2008)

Regulatory and litigation

US antitrust clearance required; closing was stated as “within three weeks upon regulatory approval” and occurred ~3 weeks after announcement. No litigation associated with the transaction identified.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Pacific Cycle became the anchor of Dorel’s Recreational/Leisure (later Dorel Sports) segment. Segment revenue grew from US$385.9m (2004 stub) to US$883.0m (2018), US$909.0m (2019), US$1,044m (2020) and US$1,172m (2021). Dorel took a US$237.2 million impairment against the Sports segment in Q4 2018. The whole segment was sold to Pon Holdings B.V. for US$810 million, closing January 4, 2022.

Cannondale Bicycle Corporation — Acquisition by Dorel Industries from Pegasus Capital Advisors (2008) and formation of Cycling Sports Group

Sponsor-to-strategic sale; strategic bolt-on creating a new channel division (independent bicycle dealer / IBD)

Announced / closed

Announced February 4, 2008 (two separate Form 6-K news releases filed February 5, 2008); closed Q1 2008

Headline value

US$190–200 million, all cash, with the final figure contingent on Cannondale’s earnings through June 30, 2008

Parties

  • Buyer: Dorel Industries Inc.
  • Target: Cannondale Bicycle Corporation (Bethel, Connecticut) and Sugoi Performance Apparel Company
  • Seller: an affiliate of Pegasus Capital Advisors, which had bought Cannondale out of bankruptcy in January 2003 for approximately US$58 million

Structure

  • All-cash purchase of Cannondale Bicycle Corporation including the Sugoi apparel brand.
  • Dorel simultaneously split its Recreational/Leisure segment into two divisions:
    • Cannondale Sports Group (renamed Cycling Sports Group / CSG) — IBD-only channel: Cannondale, GT, Sugoi, later Charge/Fabric and Cannondale-branded e-bikes. Matt Mannelly continued as President/CEO of Cannondale.
    • Pacific Cycle — standalone mass-merchant division: Schwinn, Mongoose, Roadmaster, InStep, Pacific. Alice Tillett named President.
  • GT was moved from the mass channel into the IBD division as part of the reorganization.
  • Stated objective: “to become the world’s number one IBD player.”

Financing

Debt financing (Dorel’s credit facilities). Specific facility amendments not disclosed in the 6-K. unverified

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

  • No SEC-filed PPA exists: Dorel filed Form 15F-12G on April 1, 2008, terminating its SEC registration, so the FY2008 financial statements containing the Cannondale purchase price allocation were filed only on SEDAR. Cannondale-specific goodwill/intangible allocation is therefore unverified from primary sources reachable here.
  • Subsequent segment-level impairments (see the Dorel Sports section below): US$237.2 million charged against Dorel Sports in Q4 2018.

Advisers

Not disclosed in either 6-K. unverified

Key people

  • Martin Schwartz — President & CEO, Dorel Industries — “Our intention is to build a world-class company that dealers will want to buy from”
  • Matt Mannelly — President & CEO, Cannondale — retained; “Dorel’s vast resources and experience provide us with long term stability”
  • Alice Tillett — President, Pacific Cycle division (from 2008)
  • Robert Baird — President, Dorel Recreational/Leisure segment (quoted on the 2009 Iron Horse deal)

Regulatory and litigation

No transaction-specific regulatory or litigation matters identified.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Cannondale + Sugoi combined 2007 sales were approximately US$200 million against Dorel group sales of US$1.8 billion. CSG became Dorel’s IBD arm and, together with Pacific Cycle and Caloi, formed the Dorel Sports segment sold to Pon Holdings in January 2022. Cannondale e-bikes were cited as a principal 2019 growth driver.

Dorel bolt-ons: Iron Horse (2009) and Caloi (2013)

Distressed asset purchase (Iron Horse); majority-stake emerging-markets platform acquisition (Caloi)

Announced / closed

Iron Horse — announced July 2009. Caloi — announced August 2013.

Headline value

Iron Horse — US$5.2 million plus a US$220,000 payment to CIT Group on the first anniversary. Caloi — 70% interest; price disclosed only as “a high single-digit multiple of EBITDA.” The frequently cited ~US$85m figure is unverified — Dorel did not disclose a dollar amount in the announcement.

Parties

  • Iron Horse Bicycle Company (Chapter 11, filed March 2009; secured lender CIT Group) → Dorel Recreational/Leisure
  • Caloi (Brazil; Latin America’s largest bicycle brand; Manaus manufacturing) → Dorel, 70%

Structure

  • Iron Horse: purchase of existing inventory, trademarks and patents out of bankruptcy; CIT Group to receive US$220,000 at the first anniversary and to distribute at least US$150,000 to unsecured creditors. Iron Horse had ~US$17 million of unsecured liabilities and 2008 sales of ~US$25 million.
  • Caloi: acquisition of 70%, with Caloi CEO Eduardo Musa becoming Brazil President of Dorel’s bicycle segment and joining the global management team. Put/call arrangements over the residual 30% are not described in the announcement. unverified Dorel also separately acquired the remaining 30% of Dorel Juvenile Brazil in a later transaction.
  • Post-Caloi, Dorel described combined bicycle segment revenues as “over US$1 billion.”

Financing

Not disclosed for either. unverified

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

No PPA available from SEC sources (Dorel deregistered in 2008). Caloi PPA is in Dorel’s FY2013 SEDAR-filed statements. unverified

Advisers

Not disclosed. unverified

Key people

Martin Schwartz (CEO, Dorel); Robert Baird (President, Recreational/Leisure); Eduardo Musa (CEO, Caloi → Brazil President, Dorel bicycles)

Regulatory and litigation

Iron Horse required US Bankruptcy Court approval. Caloi required Brazilian competition clearance. [unverified as to CADE specifics]

Outcome and post-deal performance

Caloi held >40% Brazilian market share on 2012 sales of R$273.5 million (+22% YoY), ~900 employees, Manaus plant capacity >700,000 units/yr. Both businesses were included in the 2022 Pon Holdings sale.

Dorel Industries — Cerberus/Koch Going-Private Transaction (2020–21, terminated)

Sponsor-led take-private / management buyout with controlling-family rollover; plan of arrangement under the Business Corporations Act (Québec); MI 61-101 related-party transaction

Announced / closed

Agreement in principle November 2, 2020; definitive arrangement agreement announced November 13, 2020 at C$14.50/share; price increased to C$16.00/share on February 1, 2021; terminated February 15, 2021 — the special meeting scheduled for February 16, 2021 was cancelled. NOT June 2021 — that date is incorrect.

Headline value

C$16.00 per share cash; aggregate purchase price approximately US$1.012 billion including assumed debt and fees/expenses of ~US$65 million. The minority equity cheque was reported at ~C$486 million (initial terms) / ~US$370 million.

Parties

  • Buyer group: an affiliate of funds managed by Cerberus Capital Management, L.P., with equity also from Koch Equity Development LLC
  • Rolling shareholders: the Schwartz family (Martin, Alan, Jeff and Jeffrey Schwartz), retaining their existing holdings
  • Target: Dorel Industries Inc. (TSX: DII.B, DII.A)

Structure

  • Statutory plan of arrangement; buyer group acquires all shares other than the family rollover shares.
  • Approvals required: 66⅔% of votes cast by all shareholders voting together, plus a majority-of-minority vote excluding the family shares (MI 61-101), plus Québec Superior Court final order.
  • Special committee of independent directors ran an 11-month process contacting 25+ potential buyers.
  • Voting record date moved to January 7, 2021 when the price was raised; shareholders had to re-vote on new proxy materials; proxy cut-off February 12, 2021 at 5:00 p.m. ET.

Financing

Cerberus and Koch Equity Development equity; committed debt financing from lenders (not named in the release).

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

N/A (no closing). Dorel expensed US$7.5 million of privatization process costs in 2020.

Advisers

  • TD Securities — formal valuation (MI 61-101), range C$14.00–C$17.00 per share, and fairness opinion
  • BMO Capital Markets — fairness opinion

Key people

  • Martin Schwartz — President & CEO, Dorel — rolling shareholder; “Independent shareholders have clearly expressed their confidence in Dorel’s future and the greater potential for Dorel as a public entity”
  • Letko, Brosseau & Associates and Brandes Investment Partners — dissenting minority holders, ~19% combined, publicly announced opposition on February 2, 2021

Regulatory and litigation

MI 61-101 minority protections; Québec Superior Court interim/amending orders obtained; no litigation. No termination fee was payable on the mutual termination.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Termination directly precipitated the strategic review that produced the October 2021 sale of Dorel Sports to Pon Holdings at a valuation the board argued vindicated the minority.

§ 07

Dorel Sports (Pacific Cycle + Cycling Sports Group + Caloi) — Sale to Pon Holdings B.V. (2021–22)

Corporate carve-out / segment divestiture to a strategic acquirer; share sale of 100% of the subsidiaries comprising the segment

Announced / closed

Definitive agreement announced October 11, 2021; closed January 4, 2022

Headline value

US$810 million (approximately C$1.03 billion); net proceeds ~US$735 million

Parties

  • Seller: Dorel Industries Inc. (TSX: DII.B)
  • Buyer: Pon Holdings B.V. (Netherlands; Pon.Bike — Gazelle, Cervélo, Santa Cruz, Focus, Kalkhoff, Union, Veloretti)
  • Business sold: Dorel Sports — 100% of the indirect wholly owned subsidiaries and related assets. Brands: Cannondale, Schwinn, GT, Mongoose, Caloi, Iron Horse

Structure

  • Sale of 100% of the equity of Dorel Sports’ indirect wholly owned subsidiaries and related assets (share deal, not asset deal).
  • Ran as a competitive sale process initiated after shareholder feedback following the failed Cerberus privatization.
  • Dorel retained two segments: Dorel Juvenile (Maxi-Cosi, Quinny, Safety 1st, Cosco, Tiny Love, Bébé Confort) and Dorel Home.
  • Use of proceeds: (i) special dividend of US$12.00 per share (~US$390 million aggregate) on both Class A Multiple Voting and Class B Subordinate Voting shares, record date January 18, 2022, payable February 1, 2022, designated an eligible dividend for Canadian tax; (ii) debt reduction; (iii) general corporate purposes.
  • Concurrently launched a Normal Course Issuer Bid approved by the TSX for up to 1,891,222 Class B shares (10% of public float) over 12 months from January 6, 2022.

Financing

Buyer-side financing not disclosed. Dorel applied proceeds to repay indebtedness.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

  • Dorel Sports was presented as discontinued operations in FY2021: revenue US$1.172 billion (+12.2% vs US$1.045bn in 2020); operating profit US$98.9 million (vs US$52.3m in 2020).
  • FY2021 total revenue US$2.93 billion (continuing operations US$1.759 billion, +2.4%).
  • The gain on disposal recognized in Q1 2022 is disclosed only in Dorel’s SEDAR-filed Q1 2022 interim statements. unverified
  • Historical Sports-segment impairment context: Q4 2018 US$237.2 million impairment of goodwill, intangibles and PP&E at Dorel Sports (part of a US$501.4 million total charge with Dorel Juvenile at US$264.2 million; consolidated impairment losses of US$525.6 million were reported for FY2018 in the FY2019 comparative). No Sports impairment in 2019 or 2020 (the FY2020 US$43.125 million goodwill impairment was Dorel Juvenile, not Sports).

Advisers

  • Dorel: Robert W. Baird & Co. (Baird Global Investment Banking) and BMO Capital Markets — financial; Fasken Martineau DuMoulin LLP — legal
  • Pon Holdings: Lazard — financial; Goodmans LLP — legal

Key people

  • Martin Schwartz — President & CEO, Dorel — “We believe that with this sale, Dorel has realized full value for Dorel Sports”
  • Jeffrey Schwartz — CFO, Dorel
  • Pon Holdings executive leadership (not named in the Dorel releases)

Regulatory and litigation

Customary antitrust/regulatory approvals; closed within ~3 months of signing, ahead of the Q1 2022 outside date. No litigation identified.

Outcome and post-deal performance

Dorel deleveraged materially and returned ~US$390 million to shareholders. Dorel Sports assets became part of Pon.Bike. Dorel continued as a two-segment company (Juvenile, Home).
