Caterpillar Inc. — Bucyrus International, Inc.

Public-to-public, one-step cash merger (reverse triangular; Badger Merger Sub, Inc. into Bucyrus); all-cash, no stock consideration; debt-financed

Announced / closed

Merger agreement signed Nov 14, 2010; announced Nov 15, 2010 / Closed July 8, 2011

Headline value

$92.00 per share cash; ~$7.6bn equity value; ~$8.6bn announced enterprise value including net debt; $8.8bn total purchase price as booked in the 2011 10-K (including $1.6bn assumed debt)

Parties

Caterpillar Inc. (NYSE: CAT) / Badger Merger Sub, Inc. (Delaware) / Bucyrus International, Inc. (NASDAQ: BUCY), South Milwaukee, WI

Structure

  • One-step merger under Delaware law; Bucyrus survived as a wholly owned Caterpillar subsidiary. Merger agreement dated Nov 14, 2010 (Exhibit 2.1 to Bucyrus 8-K).
  • Consideration: $92.00 in cash per share, without interest. Company SARs, RSUs and restricted stock treated per §5.04(a) of the merger agreement.
  • No-shop in §4.02; efforts/regulatory covenant in §5.03; conditions in Article VI.
  • Termination fee: $200 million payable by Bucyrus to Caterpillar (board recommendation change / superior proposal); no reverse termination fee disclosed.
  • Record date Dec 22, 2010; special meeting Jan 20, 2011, 11:00 a.m. CST; majority-of-outstanding vote required.
  • Vote result: 59,822,881 shares FOR = ~99.7% of shares voted, ~73.8% of shares outstanding.
  • Premium: 32.0% to Bucyrus’ Nov 12, 2010 close of $69.62.
  • Bucyrus had itself just acquired Terex Mining (closed Feb 2010), which contributed ~$1.03bn of 2010 surface mining sales — Caterpillar was buying a business mid-integration.
  • On closing, Caterpillar formed a Mining Equipment group and announced it would divest the entire Bucyrus direct-sales distribution business to Cat dealers, converting Bucyrus from direct sales to the Cat dealer model.

Financing

  • At announcement: balance-sheet cash + debt + up to $2bn of equity. The equity component was abandoned — Caterpillar confirmed at DOJ clearance (May 20, 2011) that funding would be “a combination of cash and debt” with no equity issuance.
  • J.P. Morgan Securities LLC provided committed bridge financing as part of its exclusive advisory mandate.
  • $4.5bn senior notes offering priced May 24, 2011, settled May 27, 2011 (five tranches):
    • $500m Floating Rate Senior Notes due Nov 2012 — 3M USD LIBOR + 10 bps, issued at 100
    • $750m Floating Rate Senior Notes due 2013 — 3M USD LIBOR + 17 bps, issued at 100
    • $750m 1.375% Senior Notes due 2014 — T+50 bps
    • $1.25bn 3.900% Senior Notes due 2021 — T+85 bps
    • $1.25bn 5.200% Senior Notes due 2041 — T+98 bps
    • Fixed tranches priced 99.789–99.924% of par
  • Special mandatory redemption if the Bucyrus merger did not close by June 30, 2012.
  • Ratings on the notes: A2 (Moody’s) / A (S&P) / A (Fitch).
  • Joint book-runners: J.P. Morgan, Barclays, RBS, Citigroup, Merrill Lynch, SG Americas (+16 co-managers).

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

  • Total purchase price $8,800m, including $1,600m debt assumed; net assets acquired $7,454m (2011 Form 10-K, Note on Business Combinations).
  • Acquisition-date allocation (2011 10-K, $m): Cash 204 | Receivables 689 | Prepaid 161 | Inventories 2,248 | PP&E (net) 699 | Intangible assets 3,901 | Goodwill 4,616 | Other assets 79. Liabilities: ST borrowings 24 | Current LTD 16 | AP 465 | Accrued 427 | Customer advances 668 | Other current 78 | Long-term debt 1,528 | Other liabilities 1,937.
  • Intangibles $3,901m: customer relationships ~14-yr WAL, intellectual property ~15-yr WAL, other ~12-yr WAL. Component split reported in XBRL as customer relationships $2,337m / intellectual property $1,489m / other ~$75m [unverified — XBRL extraction ambiguous].
  • Initial goodwill allocation was $5,263m, reduced by $647m of measurement-period adjustments during 2012 to the final $4,616m (2012 10-K goodwill rollforward).
  • Amortization schedule for acquired intangibles: 2012 $389m | 2013 $382m | 2014 $379m | 2015 $373m | 2016 $364m | thereafter $2,481m.
  • One-time charges: acquisition-related costs $373m; inventory fair-value step-up $303m; accelerated stock compensation $25m.
  • Stub-period contribution (July 8 – Dec 31, 2011): sales $2,524m, pretax LOSS $403m.
  • 2011 pro forma (as if acquired Jan 1): sales and revenues $62,281m; profit $5,401m; basic EPS $8.37; diluted EPS $8.11.
  • Resource Industries goodwill impairment: $595m recorded in Q4 2016, against the Surface Mining & Technology reporting unit (which carried $629m goodwill at Dec 31, 2015). Resource Industries goodwill: $3,707m (2014) → $3,565m (2015) → $2,935m (2016). Tax benefit on the impairment $17m.
  • Separately, $132m of finite-lived intangible impairment in Resource Industries (customer relationships $96m gross, intellectual property $111m gross, offset by accumulated amortization).
  • Bucyrus distribution divestitures to Cat dealers: 2013 — 19 transactions, aggregate price $467m (customer-relationship intangibles $127m, other assets $65m, allocated goodwill $56m; after-tax profit impact -$39m; Cat Financial financed $132m to five dealers). 2014 — 32 transactions, aggregate price $199m (intangibles $82m, other assets $24m, allocated goodwill $63m; after-tax impact -$22m; Cat Financial financed $20m to two dealers). Asset impairment charges of $11m (2013) and $4m (2014) on disposal groups.

Advisers

  • Caterpillar — J.P. Morgan Securities LLC (exclusive financial advisor, with committed financing). Legal: Mayer Brown LLP; Sidley Austin LLP; Howrey LLP.
  • Bucyrus — Deutsche Bank Securities Inc. and UBS Investment Bank (both delivered fairness opinions dated Nov 14, 2010). Legal: Sullivan & Cromwell LLP; Arnold & Porter LLP.
  • (Note: the working hypothesis of Goldman Sachs / Citi for Bucyrus is incorrect. Citigroup appeared only as a bookrunner on the $4.5bn financing.)

Key people

  • Douglas R. Oberhelman — Chairman & CEO, Caterpillar — deal sponsor and public face
  • Timothy W. Sullivan — President & CEO, Bucyrus International — seller-side CEO
  • Edward J. Rapp — Group President & CFO, Caterpillar — financing/capital structure [role unverified in primary sources reviewed]
  • Steven H. Wunning — Group President, Caterpillar — executive accountability for Global Mining post-close
  • Luis de Leon — Bucyrus COO → Caterpillar Corporate VP, Mining Products Division
  • Chris Curfman — VP, Mining Sales and Marketing Division
  • Dave Bozeman — VP, Integrated Manufacturing Operations Division

Regulatory and litigation

  • DOJ: HSR waiting period expired / clearance May 20, 2011 — unconditional, no divestiture ordered. The Bucyrus distribution divestiture to Cat dealers was a commercial decision, not an antitrust remedy.
  • China MOFCOM approved July 8, 2011 — characterized by Caterpillar as “the last major regulatory requirement”; no conditions disclosed in the company release.
  • No EC Phase II or conditional clearance located in the primary sources reviewed [EC clearance details unverified].

Outcome and post-deal performance

  • The single largest acquisition in Caterpillar’s history. Closed at the top of the commodity supercycle; mining capex collapsed from 2012.
  • Stub-period pretax loss of $403m in 2011 driven by purchase accounting (inventory step-up, intangible amortization, deal costs).
  • $595m of the Bucyrus-related goodwill written off in Q4 2016; $132m of intangibles written down in the same segment.
  • Distribution business divested to dealers for $666m aggregate across 51 transactions (2013–14), each transaction dilutive to reported profit.
  • Resource Industries goodwill was still $2,949m at Dec 31, 2024, i.e. the bulk of the original $4,616m survives on the balance sheet.

Caterpillar Inc. — Progress Rail Services Corporation

Corporate acquisition of a sponsor-owned platform (cash + stock + assumed debt)

Announced / closed

Announced May 16, 2006 / Closed end of Q2 2006

Headline value

~$1.0bn — approximately $800m in cash and Caterpillar stock plus assumption of ~$200m long-term debt

Parties

Caterpillar Inc. / Progress Rail Services Inc. — majority-owned by One Equity Partners, the private equity arm of JPMorgan Chase & Co.

Structure

  • Progress Rail became a wholly owned Caterpillar subsidiary, retaining its brand and Albertville, Alabama base.
  • Mixed cash-and-stock consideration to selling shareholders plus debt assumption.
  • Business at acquisition: 90+ facilities across the U.S., Canada and Mexico; reconditioned rail-car components, track materials, maintenance-of-way equipment, rail welding, scrap recycling, and repair services.
  • Management retained: William (Billy) Ainsworth and the senior leadership team joined Caterpillar.

Financing

Not separately disclosed; funded from Caterpillar balance sheet and Caterpillar shares.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

  • Acquisition-date PPA not separately disclosed in a 10-K business-combinations note at this size unverified.
  • Downstream consequence — Q4 2022: Caterpillar recorded a $925m non-cash goodwill impairment against its Rail division, “related to a lower outlook for the company’s locomotive offerings.” Tax benefit $36m. Recorded alongside $180m of Q4 2022 restructuring (largely inventory write-downs). Reported Q4 2022 EPS $2.79 vs adjusted $3.86.
  • This $925m is the entire accumulated goodwill impairment sitting in Caterpillar’s Energy & Transportation segment at Dec 31, 2024.

Advisers

Not disclosed in the announcement.

Key people

  • James W. Owens — Chairman & CEO, Caterpillar — acquirer
  • William P. “Billy” Ainsworth — President & CEO, Progress Rail — retained; later led the EMD acquisition
  • One Equity Partners (JPMorgan Chase) — controlling seller

Regulatory and litigation

Customary regulatory approvals; no conditions disclosed.

Outcome and post-deal performance

  • Platform for Caterpillar’s rail strategy: ~$2bn invested in rail/transit from 2006 through the 2010 EMD deal.
  • Jan 2020: Progress Rail sold Progress Rail Equipment Leasing Corp. / R.E.L.A.M. (rail maintenance equipment leasing) to affiliates of Paceline Equity Partners; terms undisclosed.
  • Feb 2020: BSN Sports (Varsity Brands) bought Riddell’s Kollege Town Sports unit — unrelated, noted only to avoid confusion.
  • Q4 2022: the locomotive franchise built on Progress Rail + EMD was written down by $925m.

Caterpillar Inc. — ERA Mining Machinery Ltd. / Zhengzhou Siwei (the fraud)

Cross-border public tender offer for a HKEX GEM-listed shell holding a Chinese operating company; cash + loan-note alternative

Announced / closed

Pre-conditional offer announced Nov 11, 2011; tender offer completed June 6, 2012; compulsory acquisition of the residual stub in Q3 2012

Headline value

HK$5,064m–HK$5,693m on a fully diluted basis (≈US$653m–734m), performance-contingent; originally announced as a HK$4.5bn–HK$6.9bn (US$577m–885m) range

Parties

Caterpillar (HK) Ltd. / ERA Mining Machinery Limited (HKEX GEM: 8043) and its operating subsidiary Siwei (Zhengzhou, Henan) / selling shareholders including Mining Machinery Limited (MML) and directors Emory Williams and John Lee, plus James Thompson III

Structure

  • Mixed tender offer: all-cash alternative plus a loan note alternative with performance-linked consideration (hence the HK$5.06bn–HK$5.69bn range).
  • Acquired 98.89% of ERA common stock and ~98.26% of outstanding options; compulsory squeeze-out of the remainder in Q3 2012.
  • Siwei: designs, manufactures and supports underground coal-mining roof-support equipment; founded 2003; ~4,300 employees; 600,000 m² Zhengzhou plant.
  • Existing management retained at closing — chairman/CEO Wang Fu continued to lead Siwei.

Financing

Balance-sheet cash plus the loan-note consideration alternative issued to sellers.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and impairments

  • Goodwill recognized on ERA/Siwei: $625m (2012 goodwill rollforward; total goodwill acquired in 2012 was $5,026m, comprising Bucyrus $4,616m + ERA $625m + other $387m, less PPA adjustments).
  • Q4 2012 non-cash goodwill impairment: ~$580m = $0.87 per share — announced Jan 18, 2013 via Form 8-K. Accumulated goodwill impairments rose from $22m to $602m.
  • The $580m sits in Resource Industries’ accumulated impairment of $1,175m (= $580m Siwei + $595m Surface Mining & Technology).

Advisers

Caterpillar stated it used “outside accounting, legal and financial advisors” in diligence; firms not named unverified.

Key people

  • Douglas R. Oberhelman — Chairman & CEO, Caterpillar
  • Steven H. Wunning — Group President, Caterpillar (quoted on the acquisition rationale)
  • Wang Fu — Chairman/CEO, Siwei (retained at close; Siwei senior managers responsible for the misconduct were subsequently removed)
  • Qihua Chen — Caterpillar VP; took over Siwei manufacturing post-scandal
  • Kebao Yang — Caterpillar; took over Siwei sales and support
  • Emory Williams, John Lee — former ERA directors (MML Parties)
  • James Thompson III — MML Party

Regulatory and litigation

  • Chinese MOFCOM clearance was a condition of the offer.
  • The fraud: In November 2012, during post-close integration, Caterpillar found discrepancies between recorded inventory and physical inventory counts. The internal investigation concluded there had been “deliberate, multi-year, coordinated accounting misconduct” at Siwei, with two principal mechanisms: (i) improper cost allocation that overstated profits, and (ii) early and at times unsupported revenue recognition. The misconduct began before the acquisition and continued afterward.
  • Caterpillar removed the responsible Siwei senior managers and installed new leadership. The board and audit committee review was disclosed via 8-K on Jan 18, 2013.
  • Settlement, May 16, 2013: Caterpillar and the MML Parties mutually released all claims relating to the ERA acquisition. Caterpillar’s remaining obligations to the MML Parties were reduced from $164.5m to $29.5m — a $135m reduction. An MML-affiliated company was permitted to purchase certain Siwei receivables and other assets. Caterpillar found no evidence that Williams, Lee or Thompson participated in the misconduct.
  • HKEX filings: ERA (stock code 8043) was a GEM-listed issuer; the offer documents and compulsory-acquisition notices were filed with HKEX [specific document URLs unverified].

Outcome and post-deal performance

Caterpillar wrote off ~93% of the goodwill within seven months of closing. The deal became a standard case study in emerging-market diligence failure. Siwei operations were substantially wound down thereafter.

Parties

Caterpillar Inc. / The Weir Group PLC (LSE: WEIR) — Oil & Gas division, headquartered near Fort Worth, Texas

Structure

  • Asset/entity carve-out of Weir’s entire Oil & Gas division, rebranded SPM Oil & Gas and placed in Caterpillar’s Energy & Transportation segment.
  • Scope: full line of pressure pumping and pressure control products — pumps, flow iron, consumable parts, wellhead and pressure-control equipment; 40+ global service centers; ~2,000 employees.
  • Strategic logic: pair Weir’s pressure pumping/control portfolio with Cat engines and transmissions to sell integrated well-service packages.
  • For Weir, the sale completed its exit from oil & gas to become a pure-play mining technology company.
