Transaction map

Every transaction on this file, in order

Daimler-Benz and Chrysler combined in 1998 in a three-step exchange offer and Delaware merger into a new German stock corporation, accounted for as a pooling of interests so no goodwill was ever recorded. The “merger of equals” representation produced a $300m securities class action settlement and the Tracinda trial. Chrysler was carved out to Cerberus in 2007 at a net cash outflow to Daimler, and the retained 19.9% was relinquished for no consideration in 2009.

§ 02

Daimler-Benz AG / Chrysler Corporation — formation of DaimlerChrysler AG (1998)

Public M&A / cross-border stock-for-stock "merger of equals"; German exchange offer + Delaware reverse-triangular merger into a newly formed German stock corporation (Aktiengesellschaft)

Announced / closed

Business Combination Agreement signed May 6, 1998; announced May 7, 1998 (Chrysler 8-K dated May 7, filed May 8, 1998). Exchange offer completed / combination effective November 12–17, 1998; shares began trading as DaimlerChrysler AG November 17, 1998. Chrysler shareholder vote 97.5% in favor; Daimler-Benz shareholder vote 99.9% in favor; 98% of Daimler-Benz shares tendered into the exchange offer.

Headline value

~$36bn (contemporaneous characterization of the Chrysler side; Chicago Fed retrospective: “a $36 billion transaction”). F-4 registration fee table: proposed maximum aggregate offering price $44,720,749,644 for the DaimlerChrysler ordinary shares registered. Combined 1998 revenues €131.8bn; 441,502 employees.

Parties

Daimler-Benz AG (Stuttgart) / Chrysler Corporation (Auburn Hills, MI; Delaware corporation) / DaimlerChrysler AG (“Newco AG”, initially incorporated as Oppenheim Vermögenstreuhand, renamed “Daimler Chrysler Aktiengesellschaft”)

Structure

  • Three linked steps per the Business Combination Agreement: (1) Daimler-Benz exchange offer — one Newco ordinary share for each Daimler-Benz ordinary share; (2) Chrysler merger — a Newco subsidiary merged into Chrysler, each Chrysler share converted into DaimlerChrysler ordinary shares/ADSs; (3) Daimler-Benz merger — Daimler-Benz AG merged into Newco AG, with Newco surviving.
  • Chrysler exchange ratio: 0.6235 DaimlerChrysler ordinary shares per Chrysler common share (final; the May 7, 1998 8-K disclosed a preliminary 0.547 ratio subject to an adjustment formula: Exchange Ratio = 103.4929 x (DP x DAP) + SOP / ((DAP x DP) x (DAP + SOP)), adjusting for dividends and the June 1998 Daimler-Benz rights offering proceeds).
  • Daimler-Benz exchange ratio: 1.000 DaimlerChrysler ordinary share per Daimler-Benz ordinary share in the exchange offer; 1.005 in the subsequent statutory merger (the step-up applied where pooling-of-interests accounting was achieved).
  • Ownership split: former Daimler-Benz shareholders ~58%; former Chrysler stockholders ~42% of DaimlerChrysler ordinary shares.
  • Shares outstanding used in the F-4: 647,304,195 Chrysler common shares and 569,340,329 Daimler-Benz voting shares (as of June 30, 1998).
  • Accounting condition: 90% minimum tender = pooling of interests; an 80% floor would instead have forced purchase accounting. Pooling was achieved (98% tendered).
  • Registration: Form F-4 (DaimlerChrysler AG, CIK 0001067318), filed August 6, 1998, accession 0001047469-98-029563; Chrysler side documented via 8-K/proxy (Chrysler Corporation CIK 0000791269).
  • Governance: German AG with a Supervisory Board of 20 (10 shareholder representatives + 10 employee representatives under German co-determination), of which the 12 shareholder-side seats were split 6 Daimler-Benz / 6 Chrysler per the agreement; Management Board of 18, split 50/50. Co-CEOs and Co-Chairmen: Jürgen E. Schrempp and Robert J. Eaton (Eaton contractually limited to a three-year term). Dual operational headquarters: Stuttgart and Auburn Hills.
  • Tracinda Corporation (Kirk Kerkorian), holder of ~14% (13.75%) of Chrysler common stock, signed a stockholder support agreement.

Financing

None — all-share transaction. Daimler-Benz executed a separate rights offering effective on or about June 8, 1998, the proceeds of which fed into the Chrysler exchange-ratio formula.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and accounting

  • Accounted for as a pooling of interests under US GAAP — no goodwill and no step-up of Chrysler assets was recorded. Prior-period financial statements were restated on a combined basis.
  • 1998 combined results (as reported): revenues €131.8bn, operating profit €8.6bn, net income €5.2bn (adjusted for merger-related one-time costs).
  • Share capital of the combined company post-closing: €2.56bn; >1.4m shareholders; largest holders Deutsche Bank 12% and the State of Kuwait 7%; ~25% of capital held by US investors.
  • Synergy target: €1.3bn (US$1.4bn) of bottom-line profit improvement committed for 1999, rising to “more than €3bn per year” in the medium term. Actual delivery is not separately reconciled in Daimler filings unverified.

Advisers

  • Daimler-Benz AG — financial: Goldman, Sachs & Co. (fairness opinion on the Daimler-Benz exchange ratio; consent filed as F-4 exhibit). Auditors: KPMG Deutsche Treuhand.
  • Chrysler Corporation — financial: Credit Suisse First Boston Corporation (fairness opinion on the U.S. exchange ratio; consent filed as F-4 exhibit). Auditors: Deloitte & Touche.
  • Legal (opinions filed as F-4 exhibits): Daimler-Benz AG in-house legal department, Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom LLP, and Debevoise & Plimpton. Side attribution — Skadden for Daimler-Benz, Debevoise for Chrysler — is the customary reporting but is not split explicitly in the exhibit index unverified.

Regulatory and litigation

  • Cleared by the European Commission and the U.S. FTC/DOJ in 1998 without divestitures (minimal product overlap).
  • In re DaimlerChrysler AG Securities Litigation (D. Del.): class action by former Chrysler shareholders alleging the “merger of equals” representation was false and that Daimler always intended an acquisition. Settled for $300 million in cash in 2003 — among the largest post-PSLRA securities recoveries at the time.
  • Tracinda Corporation v. DaimlerChrysler AG, Daimler-Benz AG, Schrempp, Gentz and Kopper (D. Del., Judge Joseph J. Farnan, Jr.). Kerkorian opted out of the class, sought ~$1.2bn (reported) unverified, claiming that had he known it was an acquisition he would have demanded a control premium. Claims under §§10(b), 14(a) and 20(a) of the Exchange Act plus common-law fraud (Securities Act §§11, 12(a)(2), 15 claims voluntarily dropped). 13-day bench trial; judgment for DaimlerChrysler on all counts, April 2005 (Tracinda Corp. v. DaimlerChrysler AG, 364 F. Supp. 2d 362 (D. Del. 2005)). Sanctions of $556,061 against DaimlerChrysler for late document production were upheld.
  • Third Circuit affirmed in full, September 18, 2007 (Nos. 05-2363, 05-2482), upholding the jury-trial waiver and rejecting the §14(a) misrepresentation theory.
  • DaimlerChrysler settled with its D&O carriers over the class-action payment in January 2007.

Outcome and post-deal performance

  • Chrysler Group swung to heavy losses from 2000; DaimlerChrysler’s market capitalization fell from roughly $90bn+ at combination to well below the pre-merger value of Daimler-Benz alone by 2003 unverified.
  • Schrempp publicly stated in a 2000 Financial Times interview that “merger of equals” was a presentational device — the statement that catalyzed both the class action and the Tracinda suit.
  • The combination was unwound in 2007 (below); DaimlerChrysler AG was renamed Daimler AG on October 4, 2007.

§ 03

DaimlerChrysler AG — sale of 80.1% of Chrysler Group to Cerberus Capital Management (2007)

Private equity carve-out / recapitalization of a divisional carve-out into a new JV holding company; seller retained a minority stake (subsequently relinquished)

Announced / closed

Announced May 14, 2007 (6-K filed same day); closed August 3, 2007. DaimlerChrysler AG renamed Daimler AG October 4, 2007. Retained 19.9% relinquished under a binding term sheet signed April 2009; redemption agreement executed and completed June 3, 2009.

Headline value

€5.5bn / US$7.4bn total capital contribution by Cerberus for 80.1% of Chrysler Holding LLC (enterprise-level capitalization, not cash to the seller). Daimler’s net position was a cash OUTFLOW of ~€0.5bn.

Parties

DaimlerChrysler AG (seller) / Cerberus Capital Management, L.P. (through an affiliate) / Chrysler Holding LLC (new JV holdco) / Chrysler Corporation LLC (later Chrysler LLC — industrial) / Chrysler Financial Services LLC (financial services)

Structure

  • New holding vehicle Chrysler Holding LLC owned 80.1% Cerberus affiliate / 19.9% DaimlerChrysler. Beneath it: the industrial business (Chrysler Corporation LLC) and the NAFTA financial services business (Chrysler Financial Services LLC).
  • Cerberus’s €5.5bn (US$7.4bn) was applied as follows:
    • €3.7bn contributed as equity into Chrysler Corporation LLC (industrial);
    • €0.8bn contributed into Chrysler Financial Services LLC;
    • €1.0bn paid to DaimlerChrysler (the only cash the seller received).
  • DaimlerChrysler simultaneously granted a €0.3bn loan to Chrysler Corporation LLC and agreed to compensate for the prepayment of long-term liabilities in the amount of approximately €650m.
  • Anticipated restructuring-related cash outflow at DaimlerChrysler of €1.2bn; net overall cash outflow of €0.5bn — i.e., Daimler paid ~$650m net to exit.
  • Pension and OPEB obligations retained by the Chrysler companies (transferred with the business, not retained by Daimler); the Chrysler pension plans were described as significantly over-funded at closing. Daimler retained certain guarantees.
  • Post-closing exposure: US$0.4bn subordinated loan to an intermediate subsidiary of Chrysler Holding LLC and a commitment to provide second-lien loans of up to US$1.5bn to Chrysler LLC.
  • Tax/accounting: deconsolidation on August 3, 2007; Daimler accounted for the retained 19.9% under the equity method from August 4, 2007, reported in the “Vans, Buses, Other” segment with a three-month reporting lag.

Financing

Cerberus equity of €5.5bn/$7.4bn (fund equity); the LBO debt package (~$20bn of first- and second-lien term loans for Chrysler LLC and Chrysler Financial) was arranged by JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Bear Stearns, Morgan Stanley and Citigroup and largely retained on the banks’ balance sheets when the syndication failed in mid-2007 [unverified — not in the 6-K].

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and accounting

  • 2007 expected impact: reduction in net profit under IFRS of €3–4bn.
  • Actual FY2007 as reported by Daimler AG: net loss from discontinued operations of €(870)m (Chrysler activities Jan 1 – Aug 3, 2007); Group revenue €99,399m; Group net profit €3,985m.
  • Carrying value of the 19.9% interest not separately disclosed in the 20-F unverified; written to nil in connection with the 2009 redemption.

Advisers

  • DaimlerChrysler AG — financial: JPMorgan (widely reported lead adviser; not named in the 6-K) unverified. Legal: Debevoise & Plimpton (reported) unverified.
  • Cerberus — legal: Schulte Roth & Zabel (reported) unverified. Cerberus Chairman John Snow and adviser Wolfgang Bernhard fronted the bid.
  • UAW — the 2007 deal was conditioned on UAW support, delivered by President Ron Gettelfinger.

Regulatory and litigation

  • Antitrust and regulatory clearances obtained between May 14 and August 3, 2007 (no divestitures).
  • Chrysler LLC filed Chapter 11 on April 30, 2009 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Judge Arthur Gonzalez); the 363 sale to Fiat-led New Chrysler closed June 10, 2009. Treasury’s Chrysler exposure: $12.5bn committed, of which >$11.2bn recovered, net loss <$1.3bn.
  • Old Carco (Chrysler) creditors’ committee sued Daimler in 2009 alleging the 2007 carve-out was a fraudulent conveyance that stripped value from Chrysler; litigation resolved/settled without a disclosed material payment unverified.

Outcome and post-deal performance

  • Daimler wrote the 19.9% down and, under an April 2009 binding term sheet, redeemed/relinquished the entire 19.9% stake for no consideration, completed June 3, 2009. Terms reported: Daimler waived US$1.5bn of second-lien loans extended in 2008 and made a final US$600m contribution to Chrysler’s pension plans (payable over 2009–2011), in exchange for a release of claims.
  • Net economic result for Daimler across 1998–2009: entry via pooling (no cash), exit with ~€0.5bn net cash outflow in 2007, plus ~$2.1bn of subsequent loan write-offs and pension payments in 2009.
