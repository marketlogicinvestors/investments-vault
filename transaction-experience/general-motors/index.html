General Motors Corporation — Chapter 11, §363 sale to New GM, TARP recapitalization and 2010 IPO

Government-sponsored restructuring — Chapter 11 §363(b) asset sale funded by sovereign credit bids and DIP financing, followed by an equity carve-out IPO and staged government exit

Announced / closed
  • Chapter 11 petition filed June 1, 2009, Bankr. S.D.N.Y., Case No. 09-50026 (REG), Judge Robert E. Gerber.
  • §363 sale opinion issued July 5, 2009 (In re General Motors Corp., 407 B.R. 463 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2009)); sale closed July 10, 2009.
  • Debtor renamed Motors Liquidation Company (“Old GM”); purchaser General Motors Company (“New GM”).
  • IPO priced November 17, 2010, traded November 18, 2010.
  • Treasury sold its final GM shares December 9, 2013. Canada fully exited April 2015. Ally Financial (ex-GMAC) exit December 19, 2014, closing the AIFP.
Headline value

$49.5bn total US Treasury commitment to GM; C$10.8bn / ~US$9.5bn from Canada and Ontario; $92.7bn reduction in debt and other liabilities at the 363 sale; ~$23.1bn IPO (largest US IPO at the time).

Parties

General Motors Corporation (debtor/Old GM → Motors Liquidation Company) / NGMCO, Inc. (purchaser; originally formed as Vehicle Acquisition Holdings LLC, renamed NGMCO, Inc., then General Motors Company) / US Department of the Treasury (UST) / Export Development Canada (EDC) and Canada GEN Investment Corporation / UAW Retiree Medical Benefits Trust (“New VEBA”) / unsecured bondholders

Structure

  • §363(b) sale free and clear, executed 40 days after filing, rather than a plan of reorganization. Judge Gerber found liquidation the only realistic alternative and overruled objections on sub rosa plan, successor liability (following the Chrysler precedent), recharacterization/equitable subordination of the government debt, credit-bid limits, future asbestos claims and environmental compliance.
  • Purchase price components per the Master Sale and Purchase Agreement: (i) a credit bid of the UST/EDC prepetition secured debt (~$19.4bn of pre-petition UST loans and the senior secured facilities), (ii) assumption of ~$6.7bn of DIP indebtedness plus a $1.175bn wind-down facility for the estate, (iii) surrender of the warrant issued under the Treasury prepetition loan, (iv) 10% of New GM common stock to Old GM (with an additional 2% top-up if allowed general unsecured claims exceeded $35bn), and (v) two warrants for 7.5% each of New GM common, struck at implied equity valuations of $15bn and $30bn. Total consideration was estimated by the court at ~$45bn.
  • UST funding: $49.5bn total — $19.4bn provided pre-petition (Dec 2008–May 2009) and $30.1bn provided to effectuate the bankruptcy (DIP + exit).
  • Canada/Ontario: C$10.8bn (federal two-thirds / Ontario one-third), ~US$9.5bn. C$9.8bn of loans exchanged for 11.7% of New GM common (175,105,932 shares) plus US$403m of preferred shares.
  • Equity split at emergence (July 10, 2009):
    • US Treasury 60.8% common (≈50% fully diluted) + $8.8bn in debt and preferred securities (a $6.7bn secured note and $2.1bn of 9% Series A Preferred);
    • Canada GEN Investment Corp 11.7% + US$403m preferred;
    • New VEBA 17.5% + $6.5bn of 9% perpetual Series A Preferred + a $2.5bn note payable in three installments through 2017 + a warrant for an additional 2.5%;
    • Motors Liquidation Company (for unsecured creditors) 10% + warrants for an additional 15%.
  • Warrants (as registered on Form 10, pre-split): to MLC, two warrants for 45,454,545 shares each, exercise $30.00 expiring July 10, 2016 and $55.00 expiring July 10, 2019; to New VEBA, 15,151,515 shares at $126.92 expiring December 31, 2015. After the pre-IPO 3-for-1 stock split these became 136,363,635 shares each at $10.00 and $18.33, and 45,454,545 shares at $42.31.
  • Series A Preferred Stock: 360,000,000 shares authorized/issued, $25.00 liquidation preference, 9% cumulative ($6,998m carrying value at Sept 30, 2010).
  • Debt/liability reduction: New GM began operations on July 10, 2009 with total debt and other liabilities $92.7bn lower than Old GM’s at July 9, 2009. $27.1bn of GM unsecured bonds were converted into the 10% equity + 15% warrants; ≥54% of bondholders had consented pre-petition. UAW retiree health obligations of ~$20bn (fixed obligation) were exchanged for the $2.5bn note plus New VEBA equity/preferred.
  • Old GM estate: Motors Liquidation Company plan of liquidation confirmed March 29, 2011, effective March 31, 2011, creating the Motors Liquidation Company GUC Trust (SEC-reporting, CIK 40730) to distribute New GM shares and warrants to holders of allowed general unsecured claims.

Financing

  • DIP facility ~$33.3bn from UST and EDC (the Form 10 references a Second Amended and Restated Secured Credit Agreement with UST; the court cited ~$6.7bn of DIP debt assumed by the purchaser plus the $1.175bn wind-down facility).
  • Exit capitalization: ~$17bn of debt, ~$9bn of perpetual preferred, and the common equity split above (per Treasury’s June 1, 2009 term sheet).
  • November 2010 IPO: 478,000,000 common shares offered by selling stockholders at $33.00, plus a 71,700,000-share over-allotment (fully exercised → 549.7m shares, ~$18.1bn gross); concurrent Series B mandatory convertible preferred: 87,000,000 shares at $50.00 (over-allotment to 100,000,000; $4.9bn net proceeds). Aggregate ~$23.1bn, the largest US IPO to that date. GM itself received no proceeds from the common offering. Underwriting fee reduced to 0.75%. Joint book-runners: Morgan Stanley, J.P. Morgan, Bank of America Merrill Lynch, Citi, with Barclays, Credit Suisse, Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs, RBC and others.
  • Treasury’s post-IPO stake fell to ~36.9% (33.3% with full over-allotment) of ~1.5bn shares outstanding.

Purchase price allocation, goodwill and accounting

  • New GM applied fresh-start reporting at July 10, 2009; the excess of reorganization value over identifiable net assets produced goodwill of $30.5bn [unverified from the fetched excerpt — recorded principally because certain liabilities (pensions, OPEB) were recorded at fair value while the going-concern value did not support it; substantially all of it was impaired by 2012].
  • Old GM recognized a gain on the 363 sale and reorganization items in its final period; Motors Liquidation reported on a liquidation basis thereafter.

Regulatory and litigation

  • §363 sale approved over objections from tort claimants, asbestos future claimants, dealers, certain bondholders and states; the sale order’s free-and-clear provisions were later litigated for years.
  • In re Motors Liquidation Co., 529 B.R. 510 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2015) and the Second Circuit’s 2016 decision held that the 2009 sale order’s successor-liability shield could not be enforced against ignition-switch claimants who had received no notice — reopening New GM’s exposure to pre-2009 claims (due-process holding).
  • Dealer terminations: ~1,100 US dealers wound down in 2009; Congress enacted an arbitration remedy (Section 747 of the FY2010 Consolidated Appropriations Act) restoring ~two-thirds of contested franchises.
  • SIGTARP and GAO issued repeated reports on Treasury’s exit strategy and valuation methodology.

Outcome and post-deal performance

  • Treasury: $49.5bn invested / ~$39bn recovered → net loss ~$10.5bn on a cash basis at exit (Dec 9, 2013); ~$11.2bn including the $826m still owed on Old GM’s original loan (SIGTARP/Treasury, April 30, 2014). Treasury’s own AIFP page states ~$51bn disbursed, $39bn recovered, ~$12bn net loss (inclusive of related outlays).
  • Chrysler: $12.5bn committed, >$11.2bn recovered, net loss <$1.3bn.
  • Ally Financial (GMAC): $17.2bn invested, $19.6bn recovered — a $2.4bn net gain; final 54.9m shares sold at $23.25 on December 19, 2014, ending the AIFP.
  • Chrysler Financial: $1.5bn commitment, repaid in full with interest July 2009. Auto Supplier Support Program and Auto Warranty Commitment Program: fully recovered, no loss.
  • Canada/Ontario: C$10.8bn invested; exited via 35,021,186 shares at $33.00 (Nov 2010), 30,000,000 shares at $36.65 (Sept 2013), a transfer of 36,694,915 shares to Ontario (Dec 2014), and the sale of the final 73,389,831 shares to Goldman Sachs (April 2015). Ontario realized ~C$1.1bn on its final tranche.
  • Brands: Pontiac phased out by end-2010; Saturn wind-down announced September 2009 after the Penske deal collapsed; Hummer wound down after Sichuan Tengzhong could not complete the acquisition (February 2010); Saab sold to Spyker Cars N.V., completed February 2010. GM retained Chevrolet, Cadillac, Buick and GMC in North America.
  • Plants/dealers: the June 2009 restructuring plan closed 11 facilities and idled 3 more; break-even reduced from >16m US SAAR to ~10m; US-content production share raised from ~66% toward >70%. US dealers: 5,619 at Dec 31, 2009; worldwide authorized dealerships 20,215 at Dec 31, 2010 vs 21,822 at Dec 31, 2009.
  • Delphi: GM and a lender group took Delphi out of Chapter 11 in October 2009; GM acquired Delphi’s global steering business (renamed Nexteer Automotive) and four US component plants for reported consideration of ~$2.8bn drawn from GM’s TARP funds (aggregate GM support to Delphi across 2005–2011 exceeded $12bn) [both figures unverified from primary filings]. GM completed its separation from Delphi in March 2011.
  • GMAC/Ally: separated from GM; GM’s residual interest was diluted to a minority, with Treasury holding up to 73.8% before the Ally IPO; GM sold its remaining Ally stake in 2013.
