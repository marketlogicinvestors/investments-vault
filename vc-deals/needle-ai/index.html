§04 · What the company actually does

Needle is a marketing agency delivered as software. A direct-to-consumer brand connects Shopify, Meta, Google, TikTok and Klaviyo; Needle's system reads the store's performance, brand voice and offers, and proposes a weekly slate of campaigns; the founder picks the ones to run; AI drafts the creative and a human designer in Kuala Lumpur polishes it inside a 48-hour window; Needle launches directly on the ad and email platforms and returns a weekly performance report that feeds the next cycle. Average account pays $1,646 a month.

The economic claim is not that the AI is better. It is that the bundle costs a brand roughly $20K a year against $60K to $120K for the agency it replaces, and that the human polish line inside that $20K shrinks over time as model output quality rises. The company is explicit that the human layer is "temporary scaffolding." That single sentence is the investment.

Fifteen people. Four co-founders (CEO, revenue, creatives, learnings), an AI lead and a tech lead in Los Angeles, an ops lead, and the Kuala Lumpur creative bench. The company generated 2,198 campaigns in July 2026 across 55 accounts, roughly 40 campaigns per account per month MOD.

§05 · Analogy anchor

Scale AI's economics applied to DTC ad creative, sold on an agency-replacement wedge

The analogy that actually fits is not Omneky or Jasper. It is the labor-plus-model businesses: a priced software surface in front, a managed human workforce in a low-cost geography behind, and a thesis that the human share of delivery falls as models improve. Needle even runs the same playbook geography, with the human-in-the-loop hub in Malaysia and the pricing set in USD.

Where the analogy breaks, and this is the investment question. In the data-labeling version, the buyer is an AI lab with a rising budget and the human work is measurably fungible, so automation directly converts to margin. Here the buyer is a $1M-to-$10M revenue DTC brand with a shrinking budget, and the human work is the part the customer says it is paying for. Needle's own market research is blunt about this: "They'll wait and pay more for on-brand creatives. They won't pay for slop." If the human polish is what the customer values, automating it away does not expand margin. It removes the differentiator. The company is betting that model quality crosses the threshold where the customer stops being able to tell. That is a real bet with a real date on it, and it is not in their control.

Gross margin has in fact risen from 22% (January 2025) to 67% (June 2026), so the direction of travel is established. What is not established is whether that came from automation or simply from spreading a fixed Kuala Lumpur bench across three times as many accounts. §09 separates the two.

§06 · Market, rebuilt from the bottom

The company built the honest number and then put a different one in the deck

The deck leads with 22 million ecommerce brands worldwide excluding China, 3 million in the served segment, $360B spent annually on agencies and $418B on disconnected SaaS tools CO. It then says 0.2% market share equals $100M+ of revenue at current ACV.

The company's own top-down market model, sitting in the same data room, ends at a total obtainable market of 189,369 merchants worldwide excluding China after adjusting for industry, ability to pay and internet penetration MOD. That is not a rounding difference from the 3 million figure in the deck. It is 15.8 times smaller, and it is the company's own work.

Run the arithmetic on the honest number. 189,369 merchants at the realized ACV of $19,752 is a serviceable market of $3.74B. $100M of ARR at that ACV requires 5,063 customers, which is 2.7% of the obtainable market, not the 0.2% the deck implies. The deck's 0.2% only works against a denominator of roughly 2.5 million brands, a population the company's own model already excluded.

§09 · Unit economics, rebuilt

Start with the toy version

One account pays $1,646 a month, $19,752 a year. Gross margin is 67%, so the company keeps $13,234 of it. On the trailing three months it costs $7,070 to acquire that account. The account pays the company back in 6.4 months and then contributes about $1,100 a month for as long as it stays. At the observed churn, that is a long time. These are good unit economics by any seed standard, and they are better than the category's best-funded player.

The 67% is the whole company

Gross margin runs at 66.1% (June 2026) and 68.1% (July) after LLM inference and the Kuala Lumpur human-in-the-loop bench MOD. LLM cost is trivial — SGD 809 in June against SGD 35,368 of human cost. This is not an inference-cost business. It is a payroll business with an AI accelerant.

The margin has genuinely improved, from 22.0% in January 2025 to 66.1% in June 2026. But decompose it. Human-in-the-loop cost rose over the same period, from SGD 22,099 to SGD 35,368 per month, a 60% increase. Revenue rose 236%. The margin expansion is a fixed bench spread over three times the accounts, not a falling cost per campaign. Cost per campaign fell from roughly SGD 46 to SGD 18, which is real operating leverage, but it is the leverage of utilisation, not of automation.

The projection then holds the human-in-the-loop line flat at SGD 39,550 for every month of H2 2026 while MRR grows 73%, which is what produces the 79.4% gross margin in December 2026 MOD. Seven of the eight roles the round funds are people, two of them explicitly in the human-in-the-loop function. The model funds headcount growth in the operating expense lines and assumes zero headcount growth in cost of goods sold.

The falsifiable version of the entire thesis. Human-in-the-loop cost per campaign was SGD 18 in June 2026. If it is below SGD 12 by June 2027 at roughly double the campaign volume, the scaffolding thesis is true and this is a software business with a services onramp, worth well more than a $15M cap. If it is flat or rising, this is a 67%-margin agency with good software and the terminal multiple is a fraction of what the deck implies. One number, twelve months, observable from an ordinary investor update.

The revised model of 4 September was the company's opportunity to answer this and did not. The human-in-the-loop line is byte-identical to the previous version: SGD 35,368 in June, SGD 35,550 in July, then frozen at SGD 39,550 for every remaining month of 2026. Total cost of goods sold, the LLM cost line, new customers closed, the demo-to-close assumption and the entire H2 revenue ramp are also unchanged, and the actual-versus-projected boundary still sits at July, so a month of realised August data was available and not incorporated. An updated model that revises the exchange rate and not one operating assumption is a re-save, not a revision.

The competitor that matters is not on the list. Meta has publicly stated its intention to let advertisers create and target ads end to end with AI by the end of 2026, widely reported since mid-2025 EXT. Needle's stated moat number two is "Platforms build walls. We build bridges" — cross-channel orchestration that no single platform is incentivised to build. That argument survives Meta's automation and is, in fact, strengthened by it: the more each platform automates inside its own walls, the more valuable a neutral cross-channel layer becomes. What does not survive is the creative-generation layer, and roughly half of Needle's delivered value today is creative production.

§11 · Moat under model-capability doubling

The test that matters: if frontier model capability doubles in eighteen months and generic agents get materially better at generating on-brand creative and reading performance data, what survives?

Claimed moatSurvives?Reasoning
Human-in-the-loop creative polishDissolvesBy the company's own design. This is scaffolding they intend to remove. The problem is that it is also the differentiator the customer says it is paying for. When models close the quality gap, Needle loses the reason a brand chose it over a cheaper pure-AI tool at the same moment it gains the margin.
AI creative generationWeakensDirectly in the path of both frontier models and Meta's own end-to-end ad automation. No proprietary model, no proprietary training data at scale, no evaluation harness disclosed.
The accumulating Learning LoopUnprovenThe strongest claim on paper and the one with zero supporting evidence in the data room. Cross-customer transfer at 55 accounts is thin; every competitor with more accounts has more of it. If it works it compounds; nothing shows that it works.
Cross-channel orchestrationSurvivesThe genuinely durable asset. Meta will not orchestrate Google. Klaviyo will not run TikTok. Structural incentives, not technology, keep this layer open, and better models make an independent orchestrator more useful, not less.
Workflow embedding and switching costSurvivesFive integrations plus accumulated brand voice, offers and campaign history. Real, ordinary, and consistent with 0.81% monthly churn. Compounds with tenure.
Counter-positioning against agenciesSurvivesAn incumbent charging $5-10K per month structurally cannot price at $1,650 without destroying its own business. This is the most durable competitive fact in the file and it is a business-model moat, not a technical one.

Three of six survive, and they are the three the deck ranks last. The two the company leads with are on the wrong side of the capability curve, and one of them the company has publicly committed to removing. The pitch is ordered by what sounds impressive rather than by what lasts, which is the same pattern §07 found.

§12 · Team

Founder-market fit is the best-evidenced thing about this company. Kiyan Foroughi founded Boticca, a London-based curated marketplace for independent jewellery and accessory designers, and sold it to Wolf & Badger in 2015 EXT. He then built Electric8, a profitable Singapore growth agency, and the team says it grew more than 100 DTC brands by hand before writing the software. The four co-founders split cleanly across the functions the product actually performs: revenue, creatives, and learnings. They are selling the thing they used to do manually, to the buyer they used to be. That is the configuration that most reliably produces a good seed outcome, and it is verifiable rather than asserted.

Compensation is disciplined and visible in the P&L: the CEO takes SGD 12,511 a month, the other three co-founders SGD 8,201 to SGD 11,371. The two most expensive individuals in the company are the AI Lead (SGD 25,000) and the Tech Lead (SGD 21,000), both US-based. Founders paying themselves less than their senior engineers at $1M ARR is a good sign.

The one thing to check. The diligence report notes that both the AI Lead and the Tech Lead, the two highest-paid people in the company and the two who own everything technical, have no LinkedIn profile and are identified only as Los Angeles based CO. That is unusual for senior US-based AI and platform engineers in 2026 and it is the only part of the team that cannot be checked from outside. It is probably nothing. It is also SGD 46,000 a month and the entire technical capability of the business, and it costs one email to resolve.

Fifteen people total: eight in core leadership, seven across engineering, creative and ops, with the human-in-the-loop hub in Kuala Lumpur. The round funds eight hires: two engineers, one designer, one sales rep, one media/PR lead, one account strategist, one creative designer, one finance and ops lead. The company has never had a finance lead, which is consistent with the pattern of clean accounting and loose marketing arithmetic seen throughout this file.

§13 · Go-to-market credibility

The plan requires demos booked to rise from 7 to 10 per month to 24 in October and 20 in November, on marketing spend rising from $4,651 to $10,000 per month at a cost per lead held flat at $75, with lead-to-demo conversion held flat at 60% and demo-to-close raised to 33-35% against a trailing three-month actual of 33.8% MOD.

Each individual assumption is defensible. Compounded, they ask for 2.1x the leads, 2.4x the demos and 2.7x the new customers within four months of the wire, delivered by one sales rep who has not been hired. Cost per lead held flat while spend doubles is the least defensible link: lead sources saturate, and the company's own data shows cost per lead already drifting up from $51 to $75 over fifteen months.

The deeper issue is channel mix. The book was built on referral, which is 44% of MRR at a $2,110 average, and referral does not respond to marketing spend. The plan doubles spend on the channel that produces $1,354 accounts. If it works exactly as modelled, ACV falls even as customer count rises.

This is the most testable risk in the file. New logos per month and the referral share of new MRR are both visible in any honest monthly update and need no special access.

§19 · Terms and structure

The price was right and the position is gone

A $15M post-money cap on $1.09M of ARR growing 4.2x is 13.8x current ARR and roughly 7.5x the plan's December 2026 exit run rate. For comparison, Omneky carries an $80M cap on $2.07M of revenue growing 45%. Needle is priced at roughly one third of Omneky's multiple with three times the growth rate. Whatever else is wrong with this deal, the price was never it — and the round closing $200K over target at that price is the market agreeing.

The rights package was unusual for a $2M seed round and was the strongest structural feature of the investment. It is set out below because it is what we would be buying back into at the Series A, and because the $250K threshold is the number that made it work:

MFN amendmentAny subsequently issued SAFE on better terms must be offered as an amend-and-restate. Protects against a lower cap in a follow-on tranche, which matters when the round has slipped past its own deadline.
Contractual pro rataRuns until IPO or liquidity, not just to the next round. This is the term that makes a small seed position worth holding.
Information rightsInvestor dashboard with financials, plans, and materials provided to the board. Given the quality of the accounting seen in this data room, that is a real asset rather than a formality.
Major Investor at $250,000Confers pro rata and information rights formally, and exempts the holder from the Company's right to sweep converted shares into an SPV. The remaining allocation and the Major Investor threshold are the same number.

If a $100-200K allocation is taken anyway

The committee may decide that a small position buys information and a seat in the 2027 conversation. That is a defensible view and it is not the one this memo takes, but if it is taken, three things are non-negotiable and one is not worth fighting for.

  • Fully diluted cap table with every outstanding SAFE and note, their caps and amounts, before any wire. With $2.2M of pari passu post-money SAFE paper now outstanding against an undisclosed prior stack, this is the single largest unknown and post-money mechanics make it decisive.
  • Side letter granting Major Investor rights below the threshold, or at minimum the SPV-sweep exemption and contractual pro rata. Section 7 commits only to "best efforts," which is the one drafting weakness worth a redline. The founder offered a $250K carve-out unprompted on 5 August, so the rights are clearly negotiable.
  • Corporate structure confirmation: that the Delaware entity owns the Singapore and Malaysian operating entities, the IP, and the employment contracts of the Kuala Lumpur bench.
  • Quarterly reporting including human-in-the-loop cost per campaign and net expansion excluding the largest account. Both are §15 kill criteria and neither is standard.

What is not worth fighting for is the cap. At 13.8x ARR on 4.2x growth it is already the most defensible term in the deal, and pushing on price in an oversubscribed round is how a fund spends relationship capital to save money it will not miss.

§22 · Recommendation

Pass, and stay close for the 2027 Series A. The round closed oversubscribed at $2.2M on 25 August 2026 and what remains is a $100-200K allocation below the rights threshold. This is not a company we are worried about. It is a company whose single most important number, the cost of the human layer per unit of output, has never been shown to us, whose marketing consistently overstates a set of operating results already good enough to stand unaided, and whose instrument stopped being worth buying somewhere between the fifth and the twenty-fifth of August.

Four things make this the most interesting file in the current VC series. The revenue is real, granular, and internally consistent across three independent sheets, which is rarer at seed than it should be. The capital efficiency beats the best-funded competitor in the category by a wide margin: 53% of Omneky's revenue on 18% of the capital, growing 4.2x against 1.45x. The founder-market fit is externally verifiable rather than asserted. And the terms, at 13.8x ARR with MFN, pro rata, information rights and Major Investor status at exactly the remaining allocation, are the best we have seen on a SAFE this year.

Three things hold it at 6.0. The moat is service quality and business-model counter-positioning, not technology, and the company leads its pitch with the two assets least likely to survive better models. Expansion is one account wearing a portfolio's clothing: 42 of 55 accounts have never expanded and net expansion has been approximately zero for six months. And the margin story is a utilisation effect that the company presents as an automation effect, with a plan that funds seven new people in operating expense and assumes the cost of goods sold line stays frozen.

Would I have put my own money in at the terms available on 5 August? Yes, at $250K, after four answers. Show me human-in-the-loop cost per campaign by month for the last twelve months. Show me the fully diluted cap table. Reconcile "200+ brands" against 55 paying subscriptions. And let me speak to three US customers unprompted. If the first came back declining and the rest were clean, the composite moved to roughly 7.0 and this became the best risk-adjusted position in the book, because at the full allocation it was the only seed cheque available to this fund that returned 0.9x of Fund I in its bull case.

Would I put it in at the terms available today? No. Not because $150K is too small to matter — it is, but that is the ordinary condition of a $5M fund — but because a sub-threshold cheque into a closed round buys the price without the rights, and the rights were the reason this deal was interesting rather than merely good. The correct action is to send the four questions in §18 this week while the founder still has a reason to answer them, ask to stay on the monthly update list, and hold the §20 milestones. If three of the four pass, we re-underwrite at the Series A with twelve more months of the one number that matters, and we do it as a party who was in the room in 2026 and did the work.

One thing deserves to be said without hedging, because it is the finding this memo would want carried forward. Across five weeks of correspondence, every operating number the founder narrated tied to his own ledger to the dollar, including in months where the honest number was less flattering than the round would have liked. Set that beside a deck that claims 200+ brands on a base of 55 and a demo-to-close rate the company's own forecast does not use. Those are two different failures and only one of them is disqualifying. This is the other one.

The referring GoingVC diligence report independently landed on Monitor. This memo reaches a harder call from a different evidence base, and the difference is entirely attributable to the correspondence thread and to five weeks passing. The company earned the Monitor; the calendar produced the Pass.
