§04 · What the company actually does

Smart Trainer sells restaurant operators a real-time "operating layer" that sits on top of their existing POS, scheduling and back-office systems and tells a shift-level worker what to do next: prep more dough, cover an overtime gap, reorder before a stockout. It reads three streams — what staff were trained to do, what the SOPs require, and what is happening in the store right now — and turns them into an on-screen action. A second product, Vision AI, points a camera at the make-line and grades product quality (a mis-made pizza) before it leaves the store.

The go-to-market reality is narrower than the pitch. Essentially all 118 paying stores are pizza QSR, heavily Domino's franchisees, reached through Wizardline — a data company with access to Domino's US store data. Pricing is a subscription ($199 AI/Data, $299 Vision AI per store per month on the plan; ~$75 realized today) plus a small setup fee. The company is capital-efficient: it built a working product and 118 stores on roughly $630K, which is the most encouraging fact in the file.

§05 · Analogy anchor

PreciTaste for the frontline shift, sold through the Domino's franchisee network

The useful comparison is PreciTaste: computer-vision kitchen management for QSR and fast casual, the closest funded analog to Smart Trainer's Vision AI. It locates both the value (real-time, in-the-shift guidance and quality checking) and the threat (this exact category already has players with $20–50M behind them).

Where the analogy breaks, and this is the investment question. PreciTaste and Agot raised to build the vision model as the product. Smart Trainer's real asset is not the model — better foundation models commoditize make-line vision every quarter — it is the distribution: an operator-CEO who ran Domino's stores, a lead investor who owned 80+ of them, and the Wizardline data pipe into that one franchise system. The bet is that owning a single pizza ecosystem's execution layer, cheaply, beats owning better vision generically. That can be true. It is also a bet on one brand's franchisees, one data partner, and one relationship, none of which the company controls.

The 118 stores are proof the wedge opens. Nothing in the file yet proves it widens beyond pizza, or survives Domino's corporate deciding to build or block it.

§12 · Team

The CEO's credential is the strongest and most credible part of the team, and unlike some deals in this book it is operational, not a claimed startup exit. Won Jeong reports 19 years in restaurant operations as a director of training, 155 stores directed and 250+ opened, and financial responsibility for $150M of annual revenue across Domino's, IHOP, Noodles and Paisano's CO. His lead investor, Mary Lynne Carraway, is his former boss who once owned 80+ Domino's stores. These are verifiable-in-kind operator claims, not the kind of extraordinary exit story that usually collapses under checking. That is genuinely reassuring.

The technical bench is thinner than the deck implies. The CTO, Daniel Um, is an 8-year USPTO patent examiner — a credible, detail-oriented, technical-adjacent background, but not startup engineering leadership; the "Big Data and ML deployed across federal agencies" line and the DoD/DHS/FDIC logos are not evidenced. The "senior engineers" are junior hires from Virginia Tech and a small content-AI startup (Paradym). Most consequentially, the CFO and co-founder, Andrew Yi, has left — "was cofounder, left and waiting for a later round," per the employee list. CO

So the ambitious roadmap — agentic AI, edge, AR glasses, multi-vertical expansion — sits on a two-person leadership team where the technical lead has never run production engineering at scale and the finance co-founder has departed. The distribution edge is real; the build-and-scale capacity behind it is the open question, and it is the kind a $1M round does not fully close.

Advisory board is reasonable but non-voting: MLC (lead), Derek Williamson (led restaurant-hiring software HigherMe through a 2021 acquisition), John Moses (Wizardline operations). No investor board seat exists.

§13 · Go-to-market credibility

The plan asks new-store cadence to reach roughly 74 net adds per month (118→1,000 in twelve months) funded by one new sales hire, immediately after a month — July 2026 — in which the company added zero and described itself as "focused on fundraise" CO. Observed cadence over the ramp is ~15/month, founder-led.

The credible core of the GTM is the franchise-group motion: land a franchisee, expand across their stores. The customer list is a genuine Domino's-franchisee pipeline (DMV, Happy People, Marco's, Pizza Ranch, GBR and others), and land-and-expand has visibly happened at a handful of accounts. But the same list also carries speculative entries — an "Auntie Anne camera pilot, 250 stores, $297K" — that inflate the "path to 1,000" into a number the observed conversion rate does not support.

This is the most testable risk in the file: monthly net new stores, and the share closed without the CEO, are observable from a single honest investor update and need no special access.

§19 · Terms and structure

You cannot price what has no cap

The instrument is a $1.0M SAFE. Its valuation cap is not stated on the ask slide, in the term-sheet folder, or anywhere else in the data room. Prior money — MLC and Wizardline — sits on the cap table as common stock, with the raise-history workbook referencing a "$5M post" seed. An uncapped or undisclosed-cap SAFE into a pre-revenue-scale company at a weeks-of-cash balance is not a term a committee can approve.

Worse, the same folder holds a competing acquihire term sheet. If the founders take it, a SAFE bought now could convert into approximately its cap on an early sale while the founders receive salaries, payouts and re-vested stock. The interests are not aligned, and nothing in the current structure protects the incoming dollar.

What to ask for if this is revisited

  • A stated valuation cap at or below the $5M seed reference, in writing.
  • An MFN clause and an acquisition floor (e.g. a minimum multiple on a sale below a threshold) given the live acquihire.
  • Proof the round has closed to real cash sufficient for 12+ months, before we wire.
  • Quarterly reporting including full-book gross/net retention and new-logo cadence, not ICP-filtered.
  • Reconciliation, in writing, of the ARR, pipeline and TAM figures across the deck, model and research file.

Pass on the SAFE. Stay warm on the operator. Smart Trainer is not a fake — it is a real, unusually capital-efficient product with 118 paying stores, a credible operator-CEO, and a genuine distribution edge into the Domino's franchisee network. Those facts are why the score is 4.1 and not lower, and why the call is Stay Warm rather than a hard pass.

The pass rests on four things, three of which are arithmetic. The company had $16K of cash against a $25K card balance on June 30 and added no stores in July, so the raise is survival capital on a balance sheet that may already be past empty. The instrument is a SAFE whose cap is disclosed nowhere, sitting in a folder that also contains an offer to buy the company and pay the founders — interests that are not aligned with an incoming SAFE holder. The market and traction numbers contradict each other across the company's own documents: $106K, $184K or $446K of ARR; a $300K, $25M, 200-store or 14,400-location pipeline; a $41B TAM the company's own research puts at a fortieth of that. And the one category the product most visibly competes in — restaurant vision AI — is held by players with 30 to 80 times the capital.

Would I put my own money in at these terms? No — and the deciding factor is that there are no terms to evaluate: no cap, no confirmed cash, no protection against the acquihire, and no reconciled numbers. The first three risks are ordinary early-stage risk a good operator can outrun. The last two — the uncapped instrument and the contradictory figures — are things the founder can resolve in about a week. If he closes the round to real cash, states a cap at or below the $5M reference with an MFN and an acquisition floor, and reconciles the ARR, pipeline and TAM figures in writing, this becomes a small, defensible operator bet and the composite moves toward ~6.0. That path is entirely in his control. Ask for it, and keep the relationship warm while he decides.
