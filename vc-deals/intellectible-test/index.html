§04 · What the company actually does

Intellectible converts a service company's written standard operating procedures into software that runs continuously. It watches government bid feeds against that company's real qualification criteria, tears apart RFP packages into structured data, drafts the compliance artifacts, and prices the bid using the company's own rate tables with a CFO approval gate and an audit trail. It sells first as a $24K opportunity-finding tool, then expands into pricing, which is where the money is.

The technically interesting claim is that pricing is deterministic at the core. The customer's pricing model is encoded as workflow logic and structured data; the language model extracts, normalizes, validates, and flags, but never invents the price. That distinction is what makes a CFO sign. It is also, correctly, the hardest part of the product to copy.

§05 · Analogy anchor

Deltek ProPricer for labor-driven civilian services, rebuilt as a workflow runtime

The analogy is useful because it locates the value precisely: ProPricer is the reference product for bid pricing in government contracting, and nobody has built its equivalent for services priced on geography, wage rules, facility attributes, travel, and subcontracting.

Where the analogy breaks, and this is the investment question. ProPricer exists because CAS, FAR, and TINA legally require a defensible cost basis on cost-type contracts. The requirement creates the budget line. Intellectible's civilian services buyer faces no equivalent mandate. Pricing badly is expensive, but it is not non-compliant. Regulatory mandates create budgets; process preferences create pilots. Every expansion number in this file rests on whether Intellectible's buyers behave like the first group or the second.

The HHS evidence argues for the first group. Fourteen months of data across a small cohort cannot yet settle it.

§09 · Unit economics, rebuilt

Start with the toy version

One customer pays $24,000 a year. Gross margin is 86%, so the company keeps $20,640 of it. Acquiring that customer costs $24,000. The customer must stay about fourteen months before the company is even. Everything else in this section is that sentence, scaled.

Now the real model. The existing memo reports LTV of $141K against CAC of $24K for 5.9x. That arithmetic is correct and its inputs are not comparable: it divides mature-state blended ACV of $41K, which assumes a 35% take rate on two future upsell engines, by present-day CAC, on a 25% churn assumption that a 24-month-old cohort cannot support.

§11 · Moat under model-capability doubling

The test that matters: if frontier model capability doubles in eighteen months and generic agents get materially better at reading documents and following instructions, what survives?

Claimed moatSurvives?Reasoning
Workflow runtime (Kubernetes, containerized runs, orchestration, sandboxed execution)WeakensReal engineering, roughly two and a half years of build. It is also the layer being commoditized fastest by agent frameworks and hosted execution platforms. Two and a half years of build is not two and a half years of lead in 2026.
High-fidelity document and vision extractionWeakensThis is precisely what improving multimodal models take over. The multi-pass reconciliation and schema normalization survive longer than the extraction itself.
SOP digital twin and encoded rule setsSurvivesThis is customer-specific configured knowledge, not model capability. Better models make it faster to build, which cuts both ways, but the installed base of encoded SOPs is not something a competitor can regenerate without the customer relationship.
Deterministic pricing rails with CFO approval gatesSurvivesThe strongest asset in the company. Its value comes from being not AI: auditable, reproducible, approvable. Better models do not erode a governance layer, they increase demand for one.
Integration depth and switching costsSurvivesStandard and real. Compounds in the 30 to 90 day window post-deployment.

Two of five claimed moats are on the wrong side of the capability curve, and they are the two the company leads with. The two that survive are the ones the memo treats as supporting detail. The pitch is ordered backwards relative to the durability of the assets.

§12 · Team

The technical side is strong and verifiable in kind: a CTO with fifteen years on high-performance runtime engines, a point-cloud rendering system used in broadcast, a contribution to the Apple Vision Pro team; a CPO who has delivered multi-million-dollar Salesforce implementations. They have built two companies together and have a decade-long working relationship, which genuinely reduces the most common early-stage failure mode. Founder vesting re-set at close with double-trigger acceleration is the correct structure and a good sign.

The CEO's headline credential is the single largest unresolved item in this file. The claim is zero to $10M ARR in under three years on $35M raised, exited to private equity. External data reports the prior company's 2022 revenue at approximately $940K, lists a different person as CEO, and puts total capital raised at either $24.4M or $40.4M depending on source. The business sold hardware learning kits on a product-pricing model, so describing peak revenue as "ARR" is a category error even if the peak figure is accurate. EXT

Two readings are available and only one can be true. Either the prior company hit $10M in product revenue at peak and then declined sharply before a modest sale, in which case the claim is technically defensible and rhetorically misleading. Or the $10M figure was never reached, in which case it is a fabrication. The evidence does not yet separate them, and the founder can settle it in one document. Until he does, the score treats the credential as unproven, which costs roughly 1.2 points of composite.

Board and advisor bench is credible: Armistead Whitney (founded and exited Apptega), Greg Castle (Anorak, 200+ seed investments including Anduril and Flexport), John O'Grady (Army Futures Command at Raytheon). Anorak's stated policy of not doing follow-on checks means their non-participation carries no signal either way, which the existing memo correctly notes.

§13 · Go-to-market credibility

The plan requires new-logo cadence to reach 4.0 per month from August 2026 and 4.5 by December 2027 CO. Observed cadence to date is roughly 1.1 per month, founder-led, over 24 months.

So the plan asks two account executives who have not started yet to deliver a 3.6x step-change in acquisition rate, in a market where the two best-funded competitors are hiring aggressively, while the CEO simultaneously retains head-of-sales through $3M ARR and personally owns the whale accounts.

The founder's reasoning for deferring a head of sales, that a $250K base plus $250K bonus leader is uneconomic below $3M ARR, is correct on its own terms and is a defensible capital-allocation call. The risk is not the logic. It is that the same person is simultaneously the head of sales, the whale account owner, the product direction owner, and the fundraiser, at the exact moment the motion needs to become repeatable without him.

This is the most testable risk in the file: the ratio of non-founder-led closes per month is observable monthly and needs no cooperation beyond an honest investor update.

§19 · Terms and structure

The deal is worse than the company

A priced round closed at approximately $15M post-money with a named lead, a term sheet, a board seat, and full preferred rights. The instrument on offer is a SAFE at a $20M cap, which is a 33% higher entry price for a later, less-informed, less-protected position, executed through a third party's SPV that adds a fee layer and removes direct cap-table presence.

The standard justification is access. Access is worth paying for when it buys information, influence, or allocation in a future round. Here it buys none of the three: no board seat, no information rights beyond SAFE standard, no contractual pro rata, and no direct relationship with the company because the position sits inside the SPV.

What to ask for if this is revisited

  • Cap at $15M, matching the priced round that closed weeks ago. There is no principled reason a later, smaller, less-committed dollar prices higher.
  • Direct cap-table position rather than SPV, or an SPV with disclosed and capped fees.
  • Contractual pro rata at the Series A, which is the only term that makes a small seed position worth holding.
  • Quarterly reporting with full-book retention metrics, not ICP-filtered.
  • MFN clause, given that later SAFEs at a lower cap would otherwise leapfrog this position.

§22 · Recommendation

Pass on the SAFE at the $20M cap. This is not a judgment that the company will fail. Intellectible is a real business with real revenue, an unusually strong technical team, and the best-evidenced product surface of any deal in the current series. The pricing-governance wedge is genuine and the gross margin structure is excellent.

The pass rests on four things that are all arithmetic rather than opinion. The entry price is 33% above a priced round that closed weeks earlier, with fewer rights. The probability-weighted return does not clear a seed hurdle and cannot return a meaningful fraction of Fund I even in the bull case, because the available allocation is too small and sits inside another party's SPV. Two competitors hold roughly seventeen times the capital and eight times the customers in a category that has begun consolidating. And half of the tested headline claims contradict documents in the company's own data room.

Would I put my own money in at these terms? No, and the deciding factor is the last one. The first three are ordinary venture risk that a good company can outrun. The fourth is a question about how this company describes itself under pressure, and it is unresolved. If the founder reconciles the six contradictions in §07 in writing and produces prior-company documentation, and if the cap comes to $15M with contractual pro rata, this becomes a defensible small position and the composite moves to roughly 7.0. That is a real path, it is entirely in the founder's control, and it takes about a week. Ask for it.
